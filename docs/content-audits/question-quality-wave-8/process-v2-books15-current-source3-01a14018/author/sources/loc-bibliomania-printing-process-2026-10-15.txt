L164: Let’s say the shop has been contracted to print 750 copies of a book. The printers would spend three hours printing the first forme. You might recall from the previous post that the “imposition” of a quarto meant that the forme would contain the text of pages 1, 4, 5, and 8. The printers would then spend the next three hours on the second forme, printing pages 9, 12, 13, and 16.
L165: Once the first set of sheets were dry, the printers would print on the back of that sheet the forme containing pages 2, 3, 6, and 7.
L166: This diagram illustrates a common imposition or layout for printing a book in quarto format, with pages 1, 4, 5, and 8 printed on the front of a sheet of paper (“Fig 5”) and pages 2, 3, 6, and 7 on the back (“Fig. 6”).
L167: To print on the back of a previously printed sheet, they would need to align the punctures made by the tympan points to “register” the text, making sure that the text block on the back precisely aligns with the text block that had been printed on the front of the sheet of paper.
