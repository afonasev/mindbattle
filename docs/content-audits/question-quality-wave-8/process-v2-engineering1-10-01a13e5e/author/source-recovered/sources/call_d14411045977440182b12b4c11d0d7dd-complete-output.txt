L65@P2: Mechanics 1
L66@P2: Physics is the study of matter and energy and the correlation between the two. Mechanics is a branch of
L67@P2: Physics that deals with motion. Mechanics can be divided further into kinematics (describing motions) and
L68@P2: dynamics (the causes of motion). In order to understand simple machines students need to have a basic
L69@P2: understanding of describing motion and a basic understanding of energy and conservation of energy.
L70@P2: Force
L71@P2: In physics the force is considered a push or a pull that act on an object and it will cause a change in its
L72@P2: movement, direction or shape. Net force is defined as the overall force acting on an object and it is the vector
L73@P2: sum of all the forces acting on the object. A net force acting on an object that is considered a point particle
L74@P2: will change its movement (start or stop moving, accelerate or decelerate) or will change its direction.
L75@P2: Torque
L76@P2: Torque is the rotational analogue of force. When a net force acts on a rigid body that is not considered a point
L77@P2: particle it can cause the object to rotate about a rotation axis or a rotation point (called fulcrum or pivot).
L78@P2: Mathematically, torque is defined as the cross vector of the force vector and distance vector (called leverarm).
L79@P2: Equilibrium
L80@P2: An object is in equilibrium when the net force acting on is zero. An object is not in equilibrium when the net
L81@P2: force acting on is different than zero. Static equilibrium, also called mechanical equilibrium, is the state in
L82@P2: which the net force acting on an object is zero. Dynamic equilibrium, also called rotational equilibrium, is the
L83@P2: state in which the net force acting on an object is zero and the net torque acting on an object is zero.
L84@P2: Kinematics
L85@P2: Kinematics is the branch of Mechanics that describe motion. Linear kinematics deals with motion in one
L86@P2: direction, for example free fall, while two-dimensional kinematics deals with motion in two dimensions, for
L87@P2: example projectile motion.
L88@P2: Dynamics
L89@P2: Dynamics is the branch of physics that studies the causes of motion and the effect of forces and torques on

L93@P3: Simple machines
L94@P3: Simple machines are devices that make our life easier either by minimizing the force we need to put in to
L95@P3: achieve a goal (levers, wedges, etc) or by changing the direction of the force we apply (pulley). There are six
L96@P3: simple machines. All other machines are made of at least two simple machines and are called complex
L97@P3: machines. The six types of simple machines are presented below. 2a, 3 They can be classified as "levers" (1-3)
L98@P3: or "inclined planes" (4-6):
L99@P3: 1. Lever is a stiff rod that rotates around a pivot point or fulcrum. When a downward force is
L100@P3: applied at one end the result is an upward force at the other end. A lever can either multiply the
L101@P3: applied force or the distance over which the force is applied. Depending on the relative position of
L102@P3: fulcrum, input force and output force, there are three classes of levers (see next unit).
L103@P3: 2. Pulley is a wheel with a cord wrapping around it (in a groove). The rotation of the wheel
L104@P3: (pulley) results in a linear movement of the cord, either up or down.
L105@P3: 3. Wheel and axle is a version of pulley in which the pulley, the wheel, is locked to its central axle.
L106@P3: The long motion of the rotating wheel results in a stronger, shorter motion of the axle.
L107@P3: 4. Inclined plane is a simple machine that helps lifting objects vertically by moving them across a
L108@P3: distance. A common inclined plane is a ramp.
L109@P3: 5. Wedge is a type of inclined plane that converts motion in one direction into a splitting motion
L110@P3: that acts at right angles to the "blade" (wedge).
L111@P3: 6. Screw is simple machine that converts rotational motion in linear motion (forwards or
L112@P3: backwards). Screw is basically an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.
L113@P3: Levers
L114@P3: Lever is a stiff rod that can rotate around a pivot point, called fulcrum. By applying a downward force (input
L115@P3: force or effort) at one end we achieve an upward motion (output force or load) at the other end. Depending on
L116@P3: the positions of fulcrum, input force and applied force, one can define three types of levers: first class, second
L117@P3: class and third class. The three different classes of levers and an example for each are presented in Figure 1.
L118@P3: In a first class lever, the fulcrum is located between the input force and output force; in a second class lever,
L119@P3: the output force is between the fulcrum and the input force, while in a third class lever, the input force is
L120@P3-4: between the fulcrum and the output force.Curriculum Unit 14.04.04 5 of 15

L33: 
L34: Man has always tried to use less effort when doing work. The earliest method of using less energy to perform a task was the use of the simple machines. These 6 simple machines, the lever, inclined plane, pulley, wedge, screw and wheel & axle can also be used together and make what we call a compound machine. But lets not get ahead of ourselves and begin by learning about these simple machines. This section will satisfy Performance Standards 5.1a and 5.1 f. for grades K-4 science.
L35: Remember, a simple machine does not “save” work. What we get is a mechanical advantage (how much a machine multiples the effort force). There is an ideal mechanical advantage (IMA), that we can predict mathematically, and the actual mechanical advantage (AMA) we receive when we perform the task. If you are asking why there is a difference between the IMA and AMA, it is that friction is not involved in the IMA.
L36: When we compare the actual mechanical advantage (AMA) to the ideal mechanical advantage (IMA), we can calculate the efficiency of the machine.
L37: These are the formulas we will use for our work with simple machines:
L38: 
L39: d= distance e= effort r= resistance f = force
L40: 
L41: IMA =de/dr AMA = fr/fe Efficiency = AMA/IMA X 100%

L48: The lever consists of an effort arm, fulcrum and the resistance arm. The positioning of the three components determine whether the simple machine will allow you to use less effort, gain speed and distance or change the direction of the applied force. L44: ### Prior Knowledge Required
L45: 
L46: Students should know the six types of simple machines and what they do (inclined plane, screw, pulley, wheel and axle, wedge, and lever). They should also know what a gear is (sometimes considered a simple machine or a “specialized” wheel with levers).
L47: ### Background Information
L48: 
L49: Simple machines make work easier for us by allowing us to push or pull over increased distances.
L50: “A simple machine is a non-powered mechanical device that changes the direction or magnitude of a force. In general, they can be defined as the simplest mechanisms that use mechanical advantage (also called leverage) to multiply force. A simple machine uses a single applied force to do work against a single load force. Ignoring friction losses, the work done on the load is equal to the work done by the applied force.
L51: The machine can increase the amount of the output force, at the cost of a proportional decrease in the distance moved by the load. The ratio of the output to the applied force is called the mechanical advantage.”*
L52: *Wikipedia – “Simple Machines” – accessed July 7, 2014
L53: ### Vocabulary
L54:   * Pulley – change direction of a force
L55:   * Lever – increase or decrease applied force
L56:   * Wheel and Axle – reduce friction of moving something
L57:   * Screw – hold things together or move an object
L58:   * Inclined Plane – reduce force of moving something
L59:   * Wedge- used to push two objects apart, or cut an object into pieces. It can also hold objects in place.
L60:   * Gear (sometimes considered a simple machine, sometimes a complex machine) – increase force, change speed or direction of power source – made up of a wheel and axle with levers
L61:   * Work – exertion or effort directed to produce or accomplish something; labor toil.
L62: ### Anticipated Student Preconceptions / Misconceptions
L63: 
L64: Students may mistakenly believe that:
L65: 
L66:   * Machines put out more work than people put in.
L67:   * Work is any activity one gets tired doing, paid to do, or doesn’t like doing.
