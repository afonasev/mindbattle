L26: The Starrett Steel rule is the basic measuring tool from which many other tools have been developed.

L28: 
L29: High Quality Starrett Steel Rules range in size from as small as one-quarter inch in length to many feet long. Steel rules are graduated in the English (Inch) or Metric system for both systems are provided on a single rule.

L277: cite69†Get Support L278: ## Product Overview
L279: 
L280: The primary role of flux is to remove oxides and impurities from soldering surfaces, enabling proper wetting of the solder and ensuring strong, reliable connections that maintain the integrity and functionality of semiconductor packages. Our diverse portfolio includes both flux and flux-less solutions, offering water-wash and no-clean options to meet the needs of various applications, from ball-attach to flip-chip and more.

L220@P4: Health effects of dust particles
L221@P4: The purpose of abrasives is to cut, shape, finish, clean or remove material from a workpiece.
L222@P4: In doing so, abrasive processes remove material in small amounts, resulting in the generation of dust
L223@P4: particles. Dust can also be generated from wear of the abrasive material itself during operations.
L224@P4: The finer the abrasive, the finer (smaller) the dust particles and the higher the potential risk of breathing
L225@P4: in these particles.
L226@P4: Health effects
L227@P4: Dust in the air can be hazardous to operators through contact with exposed skin or eyes, or
L228@P4: particularly by inhalation. The health effects of dust exposure are linked to the size of the dust
L229@P4: particles involved. Inhalable dusts are invisible to the naked eye. Particles less than 100μm in
L230@P4: diameter tend to be deposited in the mouth, nose and throat. From there, they can make their way
L231@P4: into the digestive tract, causing irritation.
L232@P4: Smaller particles, down to 10μm in size can be deposited in the lungs and particles smaller than
L233@P4: 4μm can be carried into the alveoli – the gas exchange regions deep inside the lungs. Small particles
L234@P4: can also be transported from the lungs to other parts of the body via the bloodstream and lymphatic
L235@P4: system. Moreover, the toxicity of materials tends to rise as particle size goes down, since the
L236@P4: relatively high surface area of ultrafine particles increases their ability to react with cells in the body.
L237@P4: Dust exposure can trigger inflammation of the respiratory system and sensitisation, leading to allergic
L238@P4: reactions such as occupational asthma. Long-term exposure to metal dusts can lead to breathing
L239@P4: problems, with long latency periods such as Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) and
L240@P4: Pneumoconiosis, as well as respiratory cancers including lung cancer.
L241@P4: For more information, see: EH40/2005 Workplace exposure limits
