L1@P0: Replace V-Belts with Notched
L2@P0: or Synchronous Belt Drives
L3@P0: Approximately one-third of the electric motors in the industrial and commercial sectors
L4@P0: use belt drives.1 Belt drives provide flexibility in the positioning of the motor relative to
L5@P0: the load. Pulleys (sheaves) of varying diameters allow the speed of the driven equipment
L6@P0: to be increased or decreased relative to the motor speed. A properly designed belt
L7@P0: power-transmission system offers high efficiency and low noise, requires no lubrication,
L8@P0: and presents low maintenance requirements. However, certain types of belts are more
L9@P0: efficient than others, offering potential energy cost savings.
L10@P0: The majority of belt drives use V-belts, which use a trapezoidal cross section to create a
L11@P0: wedging action on the pulleys to increase friction and improve the belt’s power transfer
L12@P0: capability. Joined or multiple belts are specified for heavy loads. V-belt drives can have
L13@P0: a peak efficiency of 95% or more at the time of installation. Efficiency is dependent on
L14@P0: pulley size, pulley wear, V-belt alignment, transmitted torque, under or oversizing belts
L15@P0: for load requirements. Efficiency can deteriorate by as much as 5% over time if slippage
L16@P0: occurs because the belt is not periodically retensioned.
L17@P0: The most important operational and maintenance issue in a V-belt drive is its tension. If
L18@P0: belts are too loose, they tend to vibrate, wear rapidly, and waste energy through slippage.
L19@P0: If belts are overtightened, they will show excessive wear. An increased belt load may
L20@P0: shorten bearing life through excessive lateral loading and could possibly result in shaft
L21@P0: failure. The proper tension of a V-belt is the lowest tension at which the belt will not slip
L22@P0: at peak-load conditions. After the break-in period, belt tension should be checked every
L23@P0: 3 to 6 months.
L24@P0: A notched belt has grooves or notches that run perpendicular to the belt’s length, which
L25@P0: reduces the bending resistance of the belt. Notched belts can use the same pulleys as
L26@P0: cross-section standard V-belts. They run cooler, last longer, and are about 2% more
L27@P0: efficient than standard V-belts.
L28@P0: Synchronous belts (also called cogged, timing, positive-drive, or high-torque drive
L29@P0: belts) are toothed and require the installation of mating grooved sprockets. These belts
L30@P0: operate with a consistent efficiency of 98% and maintain their efficiency over a wide
L31@P0: load range.2
L32@P0:  In contrast, V-belts have a sharp reduction in efficiency at high torque due
L33@P0: to increased slippage. Synchronous belts require minimal maintenance and retensioning,
L34@P0: operate in wet and oily environments, and run slip-free. However, synchronous belts are
L35@P0: noisier than V-belts, less suited for use on shock-loaded applications, and transfer more
L36@P0: vibration due to their stiffness.
L37@P0: Example Energy Savings Calculations
L38@P0: A continuously operating, 100-horsepower (hp), supply-air fan operates at an average
L39@P0: motor load of 75% while consuming 527,000 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electrical energy
L40@P0: annually. What are the annual energy and dollar savings if a 95% efficient (η1) V-belt is
L41@P0: replaced with a 98% efficient (η2) synchronous belt? Electricity is priced at $0.08/kWh.
L42@P0: Energy Savings = Annual Energy Use x (1 – η1/η2 )
L43@P0: = 527,000 kWh/year x (1 – 95/98) = 16,130 kWh/year
L44@P0: Annual Cost Savings = 16,130 kWh x $0.08/kWh = $1,290

L72@P1: Further Considerations
L73@P1: For centrifugal fans and pumps, which exhibit a strong relationship between operating
L74@P1: speed and power, synchronous belt sprockets must be selected that take into account
L75@P1: the absence of belt slippage. Fluid flow, pressure, and operating costs could actually
L76@P1: increase if slippage is reduced and a centrifugal load is driven at a slightly higher
L77@P1: operating speed.
L78@P1: Cost effectiveness improves when notched or synchronous belts are installed at the end
L79@P1: of V-belt life or when a belt change out is scheduled. When completing a belt retrofit/
L80@P1: efficiency upgrade, a properly designed synchronous belt drive should match the
L81@P1: required rotating equipment speed and not rely on a speed that is calculated based on
L82@P1: existing sheave diameters. Application engineers also need to understand application
L83@P1: requirements and determine the pulley center-to-center distance, operating equipment
L84@P1: speed, speed ratio, shaft sizes, and transmitted horsepower.
L85@P1: Synchronous belts are the most efficient choice. However, notched belts may be a better
L86@P1: choice when vibration damping is needed or shock loads cause abrupt torque changes
L87@P1: that could shear a synchronous belt’s teeth. Noise from synchronous belts might be
L88@P1: objectionable in some applications.
