L166 Coral Sea Marine Park
L169 # Coral Sea Marine Park
L171 South Islet (Willis Islets), Bird Islet (Wreck Reef), North East Cay (the Herald Cays) and Magdelaine Cay South are about 500 km off Queensland’s east coast. These areas are now closed to the public to protect and restore these locations.
L173 Welcome to the Coral Sea Marine Park, one of Australia’s most spectacular marine treasures.
L174 The park covers 989,836 square kilometres and has a depth range from 0-6,000m, lies off the coast of Queensland beyond the Great Barrier Reef, and is one of the world’s largest marine parks.
L176 The Coral Sea Marine Park (CSMP) has a diverse array of habitats, such as coral reefs, islands, sandy cays, deep sea plains and canyons.
L178 Far beyond the Great Barrier Reef, this marine park protects:
L179 * 16 different seafloor environments (including seamounts, canyons and plains)
L180 * 34 vast reef areas and 67 cays and islets
L181 * 15,000 km² of reef systems
L182 * an abundance of marine life.
L184 Here you can find unspoiled world-class dive sites, such as isolated reefs, where colourful coral walls are the perfect backdrop for cruising reef sharks.
L186 The park includes iconic black marlin spawning aggregations near Osprey Reef, and every year humpback whales migrate through the marine park from Antarctica.
L189 Above the water, marine turtles and seabirds breed in safety on isolated cays. Six of the world’s seven species of marine turtles can be found in the Coral Sea.
L191 This marine park is enormous (2200 km at its longest and 1300 km at its widest).
L192 The Coral Sea Marine Park is managed using National Park, Habitat Protection and Special Purpose zones.
L194 Parks Australia in collaboration with universities, state government agencies and federal partners is conducting research to inform monitoring and ongoing management of the Coral Sea Marine Park.
L218 For those who make the journey, this marine park rewards visitors with exceptional diving, boating, snorkelling, nature watching and fishing.
L220 Most people visiting the marine park travel on live-aboard tour boats from Port Douglas, Cairns, Townsville, Mackay, Airlie Beach or Gladstone, often calling in at some Great Barrier Reef sites on the way.
L222 Explore the reefs of the Coral Sea, including Holmes Reef and Bougainville Reef, each with unique features and a rich variety of species to enjoy.
