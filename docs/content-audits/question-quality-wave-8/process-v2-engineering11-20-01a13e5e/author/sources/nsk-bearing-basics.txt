L348: # Chapter 1: Bearing Basics (Uses, Types, and Components)
L349: 
L350: Let's first take a look at how bearings are used and the many types of bearings available. We'll also identify the main components found in rolling bearings and explore how they differ from plain bearings.
L351: ## What Are Bearings and How Are They Used?
L352:   1. Bearing benefits
L353: Bearings increase the efficiency of movement in machines by reducing friction.
L354: With less friction, equipment lasts longer.
L355: In this way, bearings are crucial machine components that keep equipment moving.
L356:   2. Bearing types
L357: Plain bearings (or sliding bearings) use a liquid, solid, or gas to create sliding friction between the shaft and bearing whereas rolling bearings (or rolling-element bearings) move with efficient rolling friction through the use of balls or rollers. ​

L365: ## Types of Sliding Bearings
L366: 
L367: 
L368: 
L369: ・Liquid-lubricated bearings: White metal, copper, lead alloys, etc. ・Oil-lubricated bearings (rolling bearings also utilize these lubrication methods):
L370:   1. Forced lubrication: A pump forces pressurized oil into bearings
L371:   2. Oil bath: A portion of the bearing is submerged in lubricating oil
L372:   3. Splash lubrication: Gears or other rotating equipment splash droplets of lubricating oil onto the bearing
L373:   4. Drip-feed lubrication: A lubricant container with a hole supplies a relatively constant amount of oil over time
L374: ・Water-lubricated bearings: Using water promises to be more environmentally friendly than oil.
L375: ・Gas bearings: High rotational accuracy and low friction bearings for applications unsupported by rolling bearings.
L376: 
L377: ・Magnetic bearings: Magnetic forces support the shaft.
L378: ・Hydrostatic bearings: The shaft floats on a cushion of pressurized gas provided by a pump, etc. (used in ultra-precision machining tools/measuring instruments, etc.)
L379: ・Hydrodynamic bearings: The shaft is supported by an oil film created by the rotation of the lubricated shaft (typically grooved, used in video/information equipment, etc.)
L380: ## Advantages of Rolling Bearings (vs. Plain Bearings)
L381: 
L382: Rolling bearings have six advantages over plain bearings:
L383: 
L384: 1. In general, rolling bearings offer lower friction with high rigidity.
L385: 
L386: 2. Dimensions, accuracy, markings, and so on are standardized internationally (ISO, ANSI/ABMA, JIS, etc.), making rolling bearings readily available and interchangeable.
L387: 
L388: 3. Rolling bearings are easy to maintain, replace, and inspect.
L389: 4. Depending on the type, rolling bearings can take radial, axial (thrust), moment, or combined loads.
L390: 
L391: 5. Rolling bearings can be used over a wide temperature range.
L392: 
L393: 6. Certain types of rolling bearings, such as angular contact ball bearings and tapered roller bearings, can be preloaded for high rigidity.

L429: ## Cage Roll and Types
L430: 
L431: Role of the cage
L432: 
L433: Many bearings feature cages. A cage maintains proper spacing between rolling elements and helps the rolling elements move smoothly, supporting bearing rotation.
L434: 
L435:   * Cages prevent the rolling elements (balls or rollers) from slipping or separating from the outer or inner ring.
L436:   * Cages keep the rolling elements evenly spaced.
L437:   * This prevents contact between rolling elements, suppressing additional friction and helping maintain smooth rotation.
