L414: cite182†Image L415: # 2.1—Deep Groove Ball Bearings
L416: 
L417: Ball bearings are said to account for about 70% of all the bearings used in the world. Deep groove ball bearings are the most common type of bearing, with a variety of seals and shield configurations available to extend bearing life.
L418: #### Features of Deep Groove Ball Bearings
L419:   * As the most common bearing, deep groove ball bearings are found in a wide range of applications.
L420:   * Cages are typically made of pressed steel or resin.
L421:   * They can handle both radial and axial loads.
L422:   * Unlike the linear contact found in roller bearings, ball bearings utilize point contact which allows them to support high speeds with low torque and low noise/vibration.
L423:   * The raceway groove in the inner and outer rings has a circular arc with a slightly larger radius than that of the balls.

L434: cite186†Image: Balls and the Rolling Surface L435: #### Seals/Shields for Deep Groove Ball Bearings
L436: 
L437: Seals and shields help to prevent foreign matter from entering the bearing and lubricant from leaking out. Bearings that utilize seals or shields are called “sealed bearings”.
L438: 
L439: cite187†Image: Seals/Shields for Deep Groove Ball Bearings L440: #### Features of Sealed Deep Groove Ball Bearings
L441: 
L442: Sealed ball bearings and sealed roller bearings are classified by the material and configuration of their seals/shields.
