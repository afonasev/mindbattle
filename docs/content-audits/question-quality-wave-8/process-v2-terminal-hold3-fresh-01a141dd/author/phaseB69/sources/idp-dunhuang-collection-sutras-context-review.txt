Great Departure (lines 54–62): the Abhiniskramanasutra is a copy of the Sutra of the Great Departure; it describes Siddhartha Gautama leaving the palace on a spiritual search. The page says Jnanagupta translated it during the Sui dynasty and that he translated Sanskrit scriptures into Chinese under imperial patronage.

Lotus Sutra (lines 544–554): the Chinese scroll is dated 672; the page describes 28 chapters of parables, prose and mantras, and names Kumarajiva’s widely circulated Chinese translation.

Lankavatara Sutra (lines 986–994): the bilingual manuscript is a Mahayana scripture presented as a dialogue between the Buddha and bodhisattva Mahamati; the page says it is particularly important in the Chan Buddhist tradition.

Diamond Sutra (lines 1101–1112): the page identifies the Chinese copy as a complete-with-frontispiece item and the world’s earliest dated printed book; it says the copy was sponsored by Wang Jie on 11 May 868, printed on seven joined sheets, and describes its Mahayana significance and dialogue with Subhuti. The related colophon section (lines 161–170) gives its wording: “Reverently made for universal distribution by Wang Jie on behalf of his two parents” and explains that the date is 11 May 868.
