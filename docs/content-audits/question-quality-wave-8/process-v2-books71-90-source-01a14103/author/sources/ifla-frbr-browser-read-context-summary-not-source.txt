3.1.1 Group 1 Entities, printed pp. 12–13 (PDF pp. 17–18), lines 445–459:
The Group 1 entities Work and Expression reflect intellectual or artistic content; Manifestation (physical embodiment of an expression) and Item (single exemplar of a manifestation) reflect physical form. A Work may have multiple Expressions. An Expression realizes exactly one Work. An Expression may be embodied in multiple Manifestations and a Manifestation may embody multiple Expressions. A Manifestation may have multiple Items, while an Item exemplifies one Manifestation.

3.2.1 Work, printed pp. 16–17 (PDF pp. 21–22), lines 489–509:
Work is a distinct intellectual or artistic creation and an abstract entity, with no single material object one can point to as the Work. The Work itself exists in commonality among its Expressions. It is not a particular text or recitation; it is the intellectual creation behind them. Translations are treated as different Expressions of the same original Work.

3.2.2 Expression, printed pp. 18–20 (PDF pp. 23–25), lines 562–583:
Expression is the intellectual or artistic realization of a Work in notation, sound, image, movement or combinations. It is the specific form the Work takes when realized. Physical form such as typeface and page layout is excluded. A translation into another language produces a new Expression; so does a change in form, such as notation to spoken word.

3.2.3 Manifestation, printed pp. 20–22 (PDF pp. 25–27), lines 636–672:
Manifestation is the physical embodiment of an Expression. It groups physical objects that share intellectual-content and physical-form characteristics. Copies produced as part of the same set are copies of one Manifestation. Changes in physical form, including medium (paper to microfilm) or container, yield a new Manifestation; publisher or packaging changes may do so. Changes to an individual copy after production, such as damage or rebinding, do not create a new Manifestation.

3.2.4 Item, printed p. 23 (PDF p. 28), lines 725–750:
Item is a single, concrete exemplar of a Manifestation, normally a physical object. It may consist of multiple physical objects. Individual copies can be identified and their unique copy-specific characteristics described.

4.5 Attributes of an Item, printed pp. 46–48 (PDF pp. 51–53), lines 1436–1476:
The model lists provenance, marks/inscriptions and condition as Item attributes. Provenance records previous ownership or custodianship. Marks/inscriptions include signatures, numbering and annotations applied uniquely to the Item. Condition includes differences from the Manifestation, including missing pages, brittleness and faded images.

Limit: This source establishes the FRBR conceptual model and its own examples/qualifications. It does not make every real-world format variation automatically a separate manifestation; the report's physical-form and publication-context conditions apply.
