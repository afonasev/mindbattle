L138: 
L139: Quaking aspen is a fast-growing native tree that has beautiful silvery gray bark and leaves that “tremble” in the wind due to flat leaf stalks.
L140: 
L141: Unfortunately, this tree is short-lived and prone to some disease and insect problems. It is also highly susceptible to ice storm damage.
L142: This species is native to the Chicago region according to Swink and Wilhelm’s Plants of the Chicago Region, with updates made according to current research.

L166: ### More Information
L167: 
L168: #### Native Geographic Location and Habitat
L169: 
L170: Native to the northern portion of North America. C-Value: 4.
L171: 
L172: #### Bark Description
L173: 
L174: Young trees have a grayish-white, smooth bark. Stems are a slender, reddish brown. On older trees bark is grayish white, furrowed, with darker areas.
L175: #### Leaf Description
L176: 
L177: Alternate leaf arrangement, with simple  leaves that are 1 to 3 inches wide, oval to nearly round, and have finely serrated margins. Leaves are light green, turning a bright yellow in the fall. Flattened leaf petioles allow the leaves to tremble in the wind, creating a rustling sound.
L178: 
L179: #### Flower Description
L180: 
L181: The tree is dioecious (male and female trees separate). Flowers are dangling catkins that appear before the leaves and are not ornamentally important.
L182: #### Fruit Description
L183: 
L184: Fruit is a drooping cluster of capsules containing many seeds.
L185: 
L186: ### Care Knowledge
L187: 
L188: #### Plant Care
L189: 
L190: Quaking aspen is best planted in moist soils and full sun. It is intolerant of shade conditions. The roots tend to sucker freely. This tree does not tolerate summer heat or pollution.
L191: 
L192: #### Pests, Diseases, and Tolerances
L193: 
L194: Numerous disease and pest problems if not sited correctly. This tree prefers cool summer climates.
L195: ### Cultivars
L196: 
L197: Prairie Gold® quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides ‘NE Arb’):
L198: 
L199: This cultivar is more tolerant of the heat, drought, and humidity found in the Midwest. It has a golden yellow fall color.
