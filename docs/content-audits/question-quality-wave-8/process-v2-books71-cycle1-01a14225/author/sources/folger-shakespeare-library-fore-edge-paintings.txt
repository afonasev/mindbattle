Relevant context (paraphrased): the Folger explains that a painted fore-edge can be concealed by gilt when a book is closed and revealed when the leaves are spread. It gives a historical example where, after the edges were gilded, the pages were slightly fanned and clamped before painting. The article distinguishes a standard “single” painting: one direction reveals a clear picture and the reverse only a faint hint. These details ground the hidden and single options and support the technical distinction from the double form.
