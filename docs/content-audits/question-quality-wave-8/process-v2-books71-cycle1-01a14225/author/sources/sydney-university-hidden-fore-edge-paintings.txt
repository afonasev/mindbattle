Relevant context (paraphrased): the Library describes fore-edge pictures made on the angled surface created by fanning and clamping book pages. For double fore-edge painting, it explicitly says there are “two separate images on the same fore-edge”; after the first, the pages are fanned in the other direction for a second. Its rare Deane Collection example has two views of Crystal Palace: the initial design by Joseph Paxton and the later Great Exhibition use in 1851.

The Library distinguishes split-double painting: two scenes each occupy half of the text block. This is not the same arrangement as two images successively revealed by fanning in opposite directions.
