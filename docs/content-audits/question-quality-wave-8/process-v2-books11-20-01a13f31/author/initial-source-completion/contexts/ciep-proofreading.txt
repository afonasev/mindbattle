L37 Proofreading is the final stage in the process of producing a piece of text – either in print or online – before it’s published or shared with its intended audience. A proofreader looks for mistakes within the text and also needs to take into account the effects of any changes they suggest and how they will fit into the existing page layout. The job of a proofreader is therefore to carry out the final quality check.
L42 Proofreaders do, of course, correct spelling mistakes that haven’t been picked up earlier in the writing and editing processes. But they also check for consistency across the text as a whole and adherence to style guides.
L44-56 Examples include inconsistencies in spelling; names, capitalisation and hyphenation; punctuation; spacing and line breaks; headers, footers and notes; page numbers; figures; captions; diagram text; cross-references; missing or repeated text; and contact details.
L60 Unless briefed to do so, a proofreader won’t be altering writing style, rewording sentences, rewriting headings or moving paragraphs around. Fact-checking is also not part of a proofreader’s remit.
L64 Whether it’s a manuscript for a novel, a webpage, an academic paper, or a piece of sheet music, any document or piece of text or content can benefit from proofreading.
L65 In the case of a book, the text has usually been through several rounds of editing before it reaches a proofreader.
L66 A copyeditor is usually the last person to have read the text before the proofreader.
L70 A proofreader may use Track Changes on Word, editing tools or stamps in PDF, or a paper printout with a red pen.
L98 first pass: the first time a proofreader reads something
L100 folio: a page number
L104 formatting issue: a problem with the layout of a document
L106 markup: adding comments to a document to show where changes should be made
L107 proof: a version of a document that’s being reviewed for errors; there can often be several rounds of proof before a final proof ready to be published
L109 proofreading symbols/stamps: standard marks used by proofreaders to show a typesetter what changes need making
L111 typesetting: the process of arranging text and images on a page
L113 typo: a spelling, grammar or punctuation mistake in a document
