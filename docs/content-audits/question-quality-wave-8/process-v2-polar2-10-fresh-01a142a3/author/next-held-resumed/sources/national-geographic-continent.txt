L42-L47: When geographers identify a continent, they usually include all the islands associated with it. Together, the continents add up to about 148 million square kilometers of land. To human geographers, continents are also culturally distinct. The continents of Europe and Asia, for example, are actually part of a single, enormous piece of land called Eurasia. But historically, the areas of Asia and Europe have been separated because of different cultural perceptions. Most geographers continue to divide Eurasia into Europe and Asia. An imaginary line from the northern Ural Mountains south to the Caspian and Black Seas separates Europe to the west from Asia to the east.

L144-L146: Australia is the smallest continent. The continent is sometimes called Oceania when the thousands of tiny islands of the Central and South Pacific are included.

L153-L157: Antarctica is the windiest, driest and iciest place on Earth. Antarctica is larger than Europe or Australia. Antarctica is almost completely covered with ice, sometimes as thick as 3.2 kilometers.
