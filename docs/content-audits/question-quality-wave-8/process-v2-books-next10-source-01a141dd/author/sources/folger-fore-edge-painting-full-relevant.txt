It’s technically a “hidden fore-edge painting” since the gilt edges conceal it from view almost entirely until the pages are spread open. After gilding the edges, the artist would have slightly fanned the pages, clamped them in position, then executed the painting.

While far from common, paintings have appeared on the fore-edges of books since the sixteenth century. The earliest example at the Folger is a book of engraved Bible scenes with a fore-edge painting of the arms of Oxford University and nature motifs.
