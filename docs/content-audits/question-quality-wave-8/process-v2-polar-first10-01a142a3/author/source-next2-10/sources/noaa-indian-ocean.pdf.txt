L28@P1: Explanatory Remarks
L29@P1: 0.0 Sailing Directions are published by the National GeospatialIntelligence Agency (NGA) under the authority of Department
L30@P1: of Defense Directive 5105.60, dated 29 July 2009, and pursuant to the authority contained in U. S. Code Title 10, Chapter
L31@P1: 22, Section 451 and Title 44, Section 1336. Sailing Directions,
L32@P1: covering the harbors, coasts, and waters of the world, provide
L33@P1: information that cannot be shown graphically on nautical
L34@P1: charts and is not readily available elsewhere.
L35@P1: Sailing Directions (Planning Guide) are intended to assist
L36@P1: mariners in planning ocean passages and to eliminate
L37@P1: duplication by consolidating useful information about all the
L38@P1: countries adjacent to a particular ocean basin in one volume.
L39@P1: Planning Guide publications are compiled and structured in
L40@P1: the alphabetical order of countries contained within the region
L41@P1: covered by each publication.
L42@P1: Bearings.—Bearings are true, and are expressed in degrees
L43@P1: from 000° (north) to 360°, measured clockwise. General
L44@P1: bearings are expressed by the initial letters of the points of the
L45@P1: compass (e.g. N, NNE, NE, etc.). Adjective and adverb
L46@P1: endings have been discarded. Wherever precise bearings are
L47@P1: intended, degrees are used.
L48@P1: Charts.—Reference to charts made throughout this
L49@P1: publication refer to both the paper chart and the Digital
L50@P1: Nautical Chart (DNC).
L51@P1: Corrective Information.—Users should refer corrections,
L52@P1: additions, and comments to NGA’s Maritime Operations Desk
L53@P1: or the Maritime Safety Office, as follows:
L54@P1: New editions of Sailing Directions are corrected through the
L55@P1: date of publication shown above. Important information to
L56@P1: amend material in the publication is available is updated as
L57@P1: needed and available as a downloadable corrected publication
L58@P1: from the NGA Maritime Domain web site.
L59@P1: Courses.—Courses are true, and are expressed in the same
L60@P1: manner as bearings. The directives “steer” and “make good” a
L61@P1: course mean, without exception, to proceed from a point of
L62@P1: origin along a track having the identical meridional angle as
L63@P1: the designated course. Vessels following the directives must
L64@P1: allow for every influence tending to cause deviation from such
L65@P1: track, and navigate so that the designated course is
L66@P1: continuously being made good.
L67@P1: 0.0 Currents.—Current directions are the true directions toward
L68@P1: which currents set.
L69@P1: 0.0 Distances.—Distances are expressed in nautical miles of 1
L70@P1: minute of latitude. Distances of less than 1 mile are expressed
L71@P1: in meters, or tenths of miles.
L72@P1: 0.0 Geographic Names.—Geographic names are generally
L73@P1: those used by the nation having sovereignty. Names in parentheses following another name are alternate names that may
L74@P1: appear on some charts. In general, alternate names are quoted
L75@P1: only in the principal description of the place. Diacritical marks,
L76@P1: such as accents, cedillas, and circumflexes, which are related to
L77@P1: specific letters in certain foreign languages, are not used in the
L78@P1: interest of typographical simplicity.

L80@P1: publications are in the form approved by the United States
L81@P1: Board on Geographic Names (BGN). Generally, local official
L82@P1: spellings are used for those features entirely within a single
L83@P1: sovereignty, names of countries and those features which are
L84@P1: common to two or more countries or which lie beyond a single
L85@P1: sovereignty may carry Board-approved conventional spellings

L136@P2: National Ocean Claims.—Information on national ocean
L137@P2: claims and maritime boundary disputes, which have been compiled from the best available sources, is provided solely in the
L138@P2: interest of the navigational safety of shipping and in no way
L139@P2: constitutes legal recognition by the United States. These nonrecognized claims and requirements may include, but are not
L140@P2: limited to:
L141@P2: 1. A requirement by a state for advance permission or
L142@P2: notification for innocent passage of warships in the territorial
L143@P2: sea.
L144@P2: 2. Straight baseline, internal waters, or historic waters
L145@P2: claims.
L146@P2: 3. The establishment of a security zone, where a state
L147@P2: claims to control activity beyond its territorial sea for security reasons unrelated to that state’s police powers in its territory, including its territorial sea.

L162@P2: U.S. Maritime Advisory System.—The U.S. Maritime
L163@P2: Advisory System is a streamlined inter-agency approach to
L164@P2: identifying and promulgating maritime security threats. The
L165@P2: system replaces Special Warnings to Mariners (State
L166@P2: Department), MARAD Advisories (Maritime Administration),
L167@P2: and Marine Safety Information Bulletins (U.S. Coast Guard)
L168@P2: and consists of the following items:
L169@P2: 1. U.S. Maritime Alert—Provides basic information
L170@P2: (location, incident, type, date/time) on reported maritime
L171@P2: security threats to U.S. maritime industry interests. U.S.
L172@P2: Maritime alerts do not contain policy or recommendations
L173@P2: for specific courses of information.
L174@P2: 2. U.S. Maritime Advisory—Provides more detailed
L175@P2: information, when appropriate, through a “whole-of-government” response to an identified maritime threat.
L176@P2: Winds.—Wind directions are the true directions from which
