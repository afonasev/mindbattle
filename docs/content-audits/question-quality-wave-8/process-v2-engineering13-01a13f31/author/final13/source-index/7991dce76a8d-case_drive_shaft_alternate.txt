L68: This webpage presents the design and construction of SeaDog, a proof-of-concept robot built for navigating rocky or sandy beaches and turbulent surf-zones. The robot incorporates a layered hull and chassis design, which is integrated into a waterproof Explorer Case in order to provide a large, protected payload in an easy to carry package. It employs a rugged drive train with four detachable wheel-legs and a unique tail design and actuation strategy to aid in climbing, swimming and stabilization.

L71: ### Biological Inspiration
L72: 
L73: Cockroaches have remarkable locomotion abilities that provide a wealth of inspiration to address challenges of surf-zone operation. In studies of cockroach movement, we have noted the following :
L74:   * A cockroach has six legs that support and move its body
L75:   * It typically walks and runs in an alternating tripod gait
L76:   * They adapt their gait when climbing large obstacles by using both front legs together in phase
L77:   * The cockroach turns by generating asymmetrical motor activity in legs on either side of its body
L78:   * A cockroach enhances its climbing abilities by changing its body posture
L79:   * During a climb it uses flexion joints to bend the front half of its body down to avoid high centering
L80:   * SeaDog is an amphibious robot developed for surf-zone applications. Four wheel legs and a tail provide it with excellent mobility on sandy and rocky beaches and in turbulent surf-zone areas. A rugged, waterproof outer shell and keeps its large payload area safe during operation

L87: Lobsters, which thrive in surf-zones, have an effective stabilization strategy[1].
L88: 
L89:   * Lobsters align their tail and claws with a surge, using them as a control surface.
L90:   * Forces on the body are directed downward providing more stability and friction.
L91:   * Sensors on lobster detect changes in the flow for continued stability.
L92: 
L93: Feet are optimized in nature for specific environments. We can draw from different sources and strategies to create feet for a robot.
L94:   * Lizards press long toes deep into the sand reaching compact sand below the surface.
L95:   * Crabs walk on the surface with eight legs, spreading the weight over multiple legs.
L96:   * The large surface of a camel’s foot compacts sand, creating a stable surface.

L99: ### Design and Construction
L100: 
L101: Layered Chassis Design
L102: 
L103:   * Water-resistant aluminum inner chassis provides structural integrity for the drive train.
L104:   * Waterproof Explorer Case (model 5117) acts as an outer shell with integrated seal, latches, and carrying handle.

L106: 
L107: Shaft Seal Testing
L108:   * Pressurized chamber simulates depths up to 70 meters (689 kpa).
L109:   * O-rings perform better at lower pressures.
L110:   * U-cup seals perform better at higher pressures.
L111:   * We used a combination of the two.
L112: 
L113: Torsionally Compliant Wheel-legs
L114:   * Torsional compliance provides shock absorption and enables opposite legs to come into phase while climbing.
L115:   * Linear springs are pre-tensioned making wheel legs rigid until a break-away torque is experienced.
L116:   * Springs can be swapped out to achieve desired break-away torques and torsional stiffness.

L130: Drive Train and Removable Wheel Legs
L131:   * Drive train is powerful and robust with tapered roller bearings, helical gears, and 150W Maxon Motors.
L132:   * An outer drive shaft (pink) transmits torque to and resists bending of a removable inner drive shaft (red) that connects to the wheel leg.
L133:   * With tail and wheel-legs removed, the robot takes the form of a waterproof case.
