L118@P3: A. Pokémon
L119@P3: Cards put into play to take part in battles. Players can use the attacks and Abilities written on Pokémon
L120@P3: cards, among other actions such as having them Retreat, and Evolving them by playing specific
L121@P3: Pokémon with a different name from their hand.
L122@P3: A-01 Attacks
L123@P3: ◆ During their respective turn, players can use one attack written on their Active Pokémon. Once
L124@P3: an attack has been used, that player’s turn ends.
L125@P3: If a player does not wish to use an attack and there are no other actions they wish to do during
L126@P3: their turn, they may also announce the end of their turn without attacking.
L127@P3: ◆ In order to use an attack, the Pokémon must have the right amount and type(s) of Energy
L128@P3: matching the attack cost (either exactly or more). If this condition isn’t met, the attack cannot
L129@P3: be used.
L130@P3: ◆ Steps to using an attack
L131@P3: Attacks are resolved in the following order:
L132@P3: 1. Announce the attack (“Announcing” means clearly and audibly conveying one’s actions to the
L133@P3: opponent)
L134@P3: Choose which attack you wish to use, and announce it to your opponent.
L135@P3: a. If the Pokémon you wish to attack with is unable to use attacks (or a specific attack), you
L136@P3: cannot announce the attack. In that case, you can announce that you will now end your
L137@P3: turn without attacking.
L138@P3: b. If the Pokémon you wish to attack with is Asleep or Paralyzed, you cannot announce an
L139@P3: attack. In that case, you can announce that you will now end your turn without attacking.
L140@P3: 2. Check any conditions and effects on the attacking Pokémon
L141@P3: a. If the attacking Pokémon is affected with an effect stating “When this Pokémon tries to
L142@P3: attack, flip a coin. If tails, that attack doesn’t happen”, flip a coin to check if the attack
L143@P3: doesn’t happen.
L144@P3: Heads →The attack happens: proceed to step 2-b.
L145@P3-4: Tails →The attack does not happen: your turn ends without using the attack.Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 5
L146@P4: Example: Sandslash’s Sand Attack attack (MEG)
L147@P4: During your opponent’s next turn, if the Defending Pokémon tries to use an attack, your
L148@P4: opponent flips a coin. If tails, that attack doesn’t happen.
L149@P4: b. If the attacking Pokémon is Confused, flip a coin to check if the attack doesn’t
L150@P4: happen.
L151@P4: Heads →The attack happens: proceed to step 3.
L152@P4: Tails →The attack does not happen: but 3 damage counters on the Pokémon that
L153@P4: tried to attack, then your turn ends without using the attack.
L154@P4: 3. If the attack’s text includes “Before dealing damage”, apply that part now.
L155@P4: 4. Damage calculation
L156@P4: Calculate the damage done by the attack, based on the damage written to the right of the
L157@P4: attack name. If the attack’s text mentions the attack’s damage, follow those instructions.
L158@P4: → Refer to: Damage Calculation Order
L159@P4: 5. Effects outside of damage
L160@P4: If the attack’s text mentions any effects outside of damage, follow those instructions. Unless
L161@P4: specified otherwise, apply them in the order they are written.
L162@P4: 6. Effects activating when a Pokémon is damaged
L163@P4: If your opponent’s Active Pokémon has any effects on it that activate when it is damaged, apply
L164@P4: those.
L165@P4: 7. Checking for Knock Outs
L166@P4: Pokémon whose remaining HP has become 0 due to damage or a card’s effect are Knocked Out.
L167@P4: → Refer to: D - Knock Out
L168@P4: ◆ Damage Calculation Order
L169@P4: Damage is calculated in the following order:
L170@P4: 1. The Attack’s Damage
L171@P4: a. The number written to the right of the attack name is that attack’s damage.
L172@P4: b. If the number has a +, – or × sign, adjust the attack’s damage following the instructions
L173@P4: in the attack text. If a × or – makes the damage become 0 or negative, end the damage
L174@P4: calculation there.
L175@P4: 2. Effects on the Pokémon doing the damage
L176@P4-5: If the Pokémon doing the damage has any effects on it that affect its damage, apply them to Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 6
L177@P5: the damage calculated in Step 1.
L178@P5: If an effect (from another attack or such) makes the total damage become 0 or negative, end
L179@P5: the damage calculation there.
L180@P5: Example: Premium Power Pro – Item (MEG)
L181@P5: During this turn, attacks used by your Fighting Pokémon do 30 more damage to your opponent’s
L182@P5: Active Pokémon (before applying Weakness and Resistance).
L183@P5: Example: Buneary’s Charm attack (MEG)
L184@P5: During your opponent’s next turn, attacks used by the Defending Pokémon do 20 less damage
L185@P5: (before applying Weakness and Resistance).
L186@P5: 3. Apply Weakness
L187@P5: If the type of the Weakness on the Pokémon receiving the damage is the same as the type of
L188@P5: the Pokémon doing the damage, apply the modifier written in the Weakness to the damage
L189@P5: calculated in Step 2.
L190@P5: 4. Apply Resistance
L191@P5: If the type of the Resistance on the Pokémon receiving the damage is the same as the type
L192@P5: of the Pokémon doing the damage, apply the modifier written in the Resistance to the
L193@P5: damage calculated in Step 3. If Resistance makes the damage become 0 or negative, end the
L194@P5: damage calculation there.
L195@P5: 5. Effects on the Pokémon taking the damage
L196@P5: If the Pokémon taking the damage has any effects on it that affect the damage it takes,
L197@P5: apply them to the damage calculated in Step 4.
L198@P5: If such an effect makes the damage become 0 or negative, end the damage calculation there.
L199@P5: Example: Iron Defender – Item (MEG)
L200@P5: During your opponent’s next turn, all of your Metal Pokémon take 30 less damage from
L201@P5: attacks from your opponent’s Pokémon (after applying Weakness and Resistance). (This
L202@P5: includes new Pokémon that come into play.)
L203@P5: 6. Final Damage
L204@P5: Once the final damage has been calculated, put damage counters on the Pokémon taking
L205@P5: the damage, indicating the damage taken.
L206@P5: If the Pokémon being attacked has an effect on it that prevents all damage done to it by
L207@P5: attacks, it does not take the final damage, and the damage calculation ends there.
L208@P5: A-02 Abilities
L209@P5: ◆ Some Pokémon cards have Abilities, which differ from attacks. Abilities can be sorted in two
L210@P5: broad categories: those that their player must announce to use, and those that are automatically
L211@P5: active just by having the Pokémon in play.
L212@P5-6: ◆ Abilities whose text include “you may” must be announced to be used. Unless specified Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 7
L213@P6: otherwise, they can be used either from the Active Spot or from the Bench. And unlike using
L214@P6: attacks, your turn does not end after using an Ability.
L215@P6: Example: Gumshoos’s Evidence Gathering Ability (MEG)
L216@P6: Once during your turn, you may use this Ability. Switch a card from your hand with the top
L217@P6: card of your deck.
L218@P6: ◆ Steps for using Abilities that must be announced
L219@P6: 1. Announce the Ability
L220@P6: Among the Abilities of your Pokémon in play, choose the one you wish to use and announce
L221@P6: it. If the card’s text specifies conditions in order to use the Ability, follow those instructions.
L222@P6: Example: N’s Zoroark ex’s Trade Ability (JTG)
L223@P6: You must discard a card from your hand in order to use this Ability. Once during your turn,
L224@P6: you may draw 2 cards.
L225@P6: If you can’t fulfil the conditions, you cannot use that Ability. Additionally, you can’t use the
L226@P6: Ability if going through the effects of Step 2 below would not change the game state in any
L227@P6: way.
L228@P6: 2. Go through the actions written in the Ability text
L229@P6: Follow the instructions in the Ability text. Unless specified otherwise or unless some other
L230@P6: effect applies, go through all of the actions in the order they are written.
L231@P6: ◆ An Ability whose text does not include “you may” is considered to activate automatically. Unless
L232@P6: specified otherwise or unless some other effect applies, you must go through all of the effects
L233@P6: written in its text. A player may not choose to ignore or skip the effect of an Ability that
L234@P6: activates automatically.
L235@P6: Example: Bouffalant ex’s Bouffer Ability (WHT)
L236@P6: This Pokémon takes 30 less damage from attacks (after applying Weakness and
L237@P6: Resistance).
L238@P6: A-03 Retreat
L239@P6: ◆ Retreating is when a player chooses to move their Active Pokémon back to the Bench. When a
L240@P6: player retreats a Pokémon, they choose another Pokémon from their Bench to replace it in the
L241@P6: Active Spot.
L242@P6: ◆ Retreating can only be done once per turn.
L243@P6: ◆ When a player retreats one of their Pokémon, they must discard from that Pokémon the amount
L244@P6: of Energy written in its Retreat Cost (found on the card’s bottom right corner). If no Energy is
L245@P6-7: listed in a Pokémon’s Retreat Cost, it doesn’t need to discard any Energy in order to retreat.Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 8
L246@P7: ◆ After discarding the required Energy to retreat as described above, all other Energy still
L247@P7: attached to that Pokémon, as well as any Pokémon Tools or Items attached to it and any
L248@P7: damage counters on it, stay on that Pokémon as it moves to the Bench.
L249@P7: ◆ A Pokémon can retreat even if there are already 5 Pokémon on the Bench and no more open
L250@P7: Bench slots. A Pokémon can’t retreat, however, if there are no Benched Pokémon.
L251@P7: ◆ When an Active Pokémon moves to the Bench, it loses all Special Conditions and any effects it
L252@P7: had on it (such as “This Pokémon can’t attack during your next turn”).
L253@P7: ◆ Steps for Retreating
L254@P7: 1. Announce that you will retreat your Active Pokémon.
L255@P7: a. If a Pokémon doesn’t have at least as much Energy attached to it as written in its
L256@P7: Retreat Cost, it can’t retreat.
L257@P7: b. If a Pokémon is Asleep or Paralyzed, it can’t retreat.
L258@P7: c. Some effects of cards may prevent an Active Pokémon from retreating.
L259@P7: 2. Discard from your Active Pokémon an amount of Energy that matches its Retreat Cost.
L260@P7: 3. Move your Active Pokémon back to your Bench, and send another of your Benched Pokémon
L261@P7: to the Active Spot.
L262@P7: A-04 Putting onto the Bench
L263@P7: ◆ Basic Pokémon can be played from the hand onto the Bench.
L264@P7: ◆ A player can play a Basic Pokémon from their hand onto their Bench as many times as they
L265@P7: want during their turn, within the limit of 5 Pokémon on each player’s Bench.
L266@P7: ◆ Pokémon must be put onto the Bench from the hand one by one. Players can’t put several
L267@P7: Pokémon from their hand onto their Bench at once.
L268@P7: A-05 Evolution
L269@P7: ◆ If a player has an Evolution Pokémon in their hand that evolves from one of their Pokémon in
L270@P7: play, they may play it from their hand on top of that Pokémon to evolve it. The upper-left corner
L271@P7: of an Evolution Pokémon specifies its Stage and which Pokémon it evolves from.
L272@P7: ◆ A Pokémon can Evolve whether it’s in the Active Spot or on the Bench. A player may evolve any
L273@P7-8: number of their Pokémon during their turn.Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 9
L274@P8: ◆ Neither player can evolve any of their Pokémon during their first turn. Likewise, a Basic
L275@P8: Pokémon that was just put into play cannot evolve that turn, and a Pokémon that just evolved
L276@P8: cannot evolve further that turn.
L277@P8: ◆ When a Pokémon evolves, it keeps all attached cards (Energy, Pokémon Tools, Items) and all
L278@P8: damage counters it had before evolving.
L279@P8: ◆ When an Active Pokémon evolves, any Special Conditions or effects of attacks it had on it (such
L280@P8: as “This Pokémon can’t attack during your next turn”) disappears after evolving.
L281@P8: ◆ An evolved Pokémon cannot use the attacks or Abilities written on its previous Stage Pokémon.
L282@P8: B. Trainer Cards
L283@P8: Trainer cards have useful effects to help players and their Pokémon in battle. There are four main
L284@P8: types of Trainer cards: Items, Pokémon Tools, Supporters, and Stadiums – and each card has its own
L285@P8: specific use and effect.
L286@P8: B-01 Items
L287@P8: ◆ A type of Trainer card, players can use any number of Item cards during their turn. Once used,
L288@P8: Items get discarded.
L289@P8: ◆ Steps for using Items
L290@P8: 1. If the Item’s text specifies conditions required in order to use it, follow those instructions.
L291@P8: If you can’t satisfy those conditions, you cannot use that Item. Additionally, you can’t use that
L292@P8: Item if going through the actions of Step 3 below would not change the game state in any
L293@P8: way.
L294@P8: 2. Choose the Item you wish to play from your hand, and reveal it to your opponent to play it.
L295@P8: 3. Follow the instructions in the Item’s text. Unless specified otherwise, go through all the
L296@P8: actions in the order they are written.
L297@P8: 4. Once the Item has been used, discard it.
L298@P8: B-02 Pokémon Tools
L299@P8: ◆ A type of Trainer card, players can play any number of Pokémon Tool cards during their turn.
L300@P8: ◆ Pokémon Tools can be attached to Pokémon – a Pokémon can only have one Pokémon Tool
L301@P8: attached at a time. While it is attached to a Pokémon, a Pokémon Tool’s effect as described in its
L302@P8: text is automatically active. Unless specified otherwise, a Pokémon Tool remains attached to its
L303@P8-9: Pokémon until that Pokémon gets Knocked Out, at which point they both get discarded. When a Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 10
L304@P9: Pokémon evolves, or moves to or from the Bench, it keeps its Pokémon Tool attached.
L305@P9: Example: Air Balloon – Pokémon Tool (BLK)
L306@P9: The Retreat Cost of the Pokémon this card is attached to is 2 Colorless less.
L307@P9: ◆ Unless specified otherwise, a Pokémon Tool’s full effect always applies. A Pokémon Tool’s effect
L308@P9: is automatically active, and a player may not choose to ignore or skip part (or all) of it.
L309@P9: ◆ All of this also applies to Pokémon Tool cards from the Sword & Shield series and before, which
L310@P9: had “Item” written on the top of the card. Despite that text, these are not treated as Items.
L311@P9: ◆ Players may not freely remove a Pokémon Tool that has been attached to a Pokémon. They
L312@P9: cannot replace it with another Pokémon Tool from their hand either.
L313@P9: B-03 Supporters
L314@P9: ◆ A type of Trainer card, players may only use one Supporter during their turn. Once a Supporter
L315@P9: has been used, it is discarded.
L316@P9: ◆ Steps for using a Supporter card
L317@P9: 1. If the text of the Supporter you wish to use specifies conditions required in order to use it,
L318@P9: follow those instructions. If you can’t satisfy those conditions, you cannot use that Supporter.
L319@P9: Additionally, you cannot use that Supporter if going through the actions of Step 3 below
L320@P9: would not change the game state in any way.
L321@P9: 2. Choose the Supporter you wish to play from your hand, and reveal it to your opponent to play
L322@P9: it.
L323@P9: 3. Follow the instructions in the Supporter’s text. Unless specified otherwise or unless some
L324@P9: other effect applies, go through all of the actions in the order they are written.
L325@P9: 4. Once the supporter has been used, discard it.
L326@P9: B-04 Stadiums
L327@P9: ◆ A type of Trainer card, Stadiums can be put into play and stay there, continually affecting both
L328@P9: players and their Pokémon. Players may only play one Stadium during their turn.
L329@P9: ◆ Once a Stadium has been played from the hand, it stays in play. If either player then plays a
L330@P9: Stadium with a different name, it replaces the previous Stadium, which gets discarded. Players
L331@P9: can’t play a Stadium of the same name as the one currently in play.
L332@P9: ◆ The effects of Stadiums can be sorted into two broad categories: effects that players must
L333@P9: announce to use voluntarily, and those that are automatically active just by having the Stadium
L334@P9-10: in play. If a Stadium’s text includes “that player may…”, each player can choose to announce and Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 11
L335@P10: use that effect during their turn.
L336@P10: Example: Mystery Garden – Stadium (MEG)
L337@P10: Once during each player’s turn, that player may discard an Energy card from their hand in
L338@P10: order to draw cards until they have as many cards in their hand as they have Psychic
L339@P10: Pokémon in play.
L340@P10: ◆ Steps to announcing and using a Stadium's effect
L341@P10: 1. If the text of the Stadium effect you wish to use specifies conditions required in order to use
L342@P10: it, follow those instructions. If you can’t satisfy those conditions, you cannot use the effect of
L343@P10: that Stadium. Additionally, you can’t use the effect of that Stadium if going through the
L344@P10: actions of Step 3 below would not change the game state in any way.
L345@P10: 2. Announce that you are using the effect of the Stadium.
L346@P10: 3. Follow the instructions in the Stadium’s text. Unless specified otherwise or unless some other
L347@P10: effect applies, go through all of the actions in the order they are written.
L348@P10: ◆ The effect of a Stadium whose text does not include a variation of “that player may…” is
L349@P10: automatically active. Unless specified otherwise, or unless some other effect comes into play, the
L350@P10: full effect of the Stadium always applies. Players may not choose to ignore or skip part (or all) of
L351@P10: the automatic effect of such a Stadium.
L352@P10: Example: Team Rocket’s Watchtower – Stadium (DRI)
L353@P10: Colorless Pokémon in play (both yours and your opponent’s) have no Abilities.
L354@P10: C. Energy Cards
L355@P10: ◆ Energy Cards allow Pokémon to use their attacks or retreat. There are two categories of Energy
L356@P10: cards: Basic Energy and Special Energy.
L357@P10: ◆ Once during their turn, players may attach one Energy card from their hand to one of their
L358@P10: Pokémon. There is no limit to the total number of Energy cards a Pokémon may have on it.
L359@P10: ◆ Unless specified otherwise, Energy stays attached to a Pokémon after it has used an attack, as
L360@P10: well as when it evolves. Energy remains attached to its Pokémon until that Pokémon gets
L361@P10: Knocked Out, at which point both the Pokémon and its Energy get discarded.
L362@P10: ◆ Basic Energy can be one of 9 types : Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness,
L363@P10: Metal, and Fairy. They are identified by their respective name and symbol. One Basic Energy card
L364@P10: provides one Energy of its type to the Pokémon it is attached to. The deck building rule of “only
L365@P10: up to 4 cards of the same name” does not apply to Basic Energy, which players can have any
L366@P10-11: number of in their decks.Pokémon Card Game Advanced Player’s Rulebook Ver. 3.4 12
L367@P11: ◆ Special Energy cards have “Special Energy” written on the top part of the card. They also have
L368@P11: their own specific effects and uses. Unlike with Basic Energy, the deck building rule of “only up
L369@P11: to 4 cards of the same name” does apply to Special Energy.
L370@P11: D. Knock Out
L371@P11: ◆ If any Pokémon on either player’s side has its remaining HP reduced to zero by damage from an
L372@P11: attack, by an effect of a card, by a Special Condition or for any other reason, it is Knocked Out: in
L373@P11: such cases, go through the Knock Out process.
L374@P11: ◆ When to check for a Knock Out
L375@P11: ● At the end of an attack
L376@P11: ● At the end of Pokémon checkup
L377@P11: ● Anytime a Pokémon’s remaining HP becomes zero, in addition to the above
L378@P11: Example: Team Rocket’s Crobat’s Biting Spree Ability (DRI)
L379@P11: When you play this Pokémon from your hand to evolve 1 of your Pokémon during your
L380@P11: turn, you may choose 2 of your opponent’s Pokémon and put 2 damage counters on each
L381@P11: of them.
L382@P11: Example: Team Rocket’s Venture Bomb – Item (DRI)
L383@P11: Flip a coin. If heads, put 2 damage counters on 1 of your opponent’s Pokémon. If tails, put
L384@P11: 2 damage counters on your Active Pokémon.
