L1145: ## Britain's most famous castle
L1146: 
L1147: Instantly recognisable, the White Tower is the most famous castle keep in the world. It was built between 1078-1100 by cite97†William the Conqueror to awe, subdue and terrify Londoners, and to deter foreign invaders. It is an iconic symbol of London and Britain.
L1148: Along with the rest of the Tower fortress, the White Tower is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a Scheduled Monument. Each floor is packed with extraordinary exhibits showing how this impressive building, and the Tower at large, has been used through the centuries.
L1149: 
L1150: Hear the fascinating stories of the many important institutions that were based at the Tower of London, from the Board of Ordnance to the cite54†Royal Mint .
L1151: As you climb the steps to enter the White Tower, don’t miss the plaque marking the spot where the remains of the Princes in the Tower were said to be found in 1674, before being moved to Westminster Abbey.

L1168: ## Norman fortress
L1169: 
L1170: With its thick, fortified walls, tall turrets and winding staircases, the White Tower remains as formidable today as when it was originally built.
L1171: 
L1172: Look out for distinctive Norman features as you explore, including an innovative fireplace, garderobes (medieval toilets), and the well in the basement - an essential lifeline during times of siege.
L1173: This fortress is also a place of luxury. Witness the breathtaking 11th-century cite51†Chapel of St John the Evangelist , a unique Romanesque chapel built for kings and queens as a private place of worship. With its high ceiling, grand archways and ethereal atmosphere, this space can transport you back to the 11th century - and it is still used as a working chapel today!

L1227: ## Torture at the Tower
L1228: 
L1229: The basement of the White Tower is thought to be the site of the torture and interrogation of prisoners such as cite74†Guy Fawkes and the Jesuit Priest John Gerard.
L1230: 
L1231: Not for the faint-hearted, various instruments of torture are displayed on the second floor. Read more about these gruesome cite526†Tools of Torment†royalarmouries.org on the Royal Armouries website.
L1232: An original executioner's block of the 18th century can also be found in the basement, with an axe that possibly dates back to Tudor times. They were once incorrectly labelled as the implements used to behead cite63†Anne Boleyn . However, she was actually executed by sword.
L1233: 
L1234: It is now believed that the block and axe were used at the last public beheading on Tower Hill in 1747.

L1237: ## Fearsome prison
L1238: 
L1239: For at least 400 years, the White Tower occasionally served as a prison. Its earliest recorded inmate was also its first escapee - Rannulf Flambard, Bishop of Durham, in 1101. Famously he distracted his guards with a banquet and smuggled a line of rope into his room in a barrel of wine.
L1240: 
L1241: Other notable prisoners include the Welsh prince Gruffudd ap Llywelyn, King John II of France, King James I of Scotland and Charles, Duke of Orléans.
L1242: The White Tower’s role as a prison declined during the sixteenth century but many were cite92†imprisoned in other Tower buildings and the basement continued to be used as a place of torture.
L1243: Executions at the Tower did not cease until the 20th century. A German spy, cite527†Josef Jakobs , the last person to be executed at the Tower, was held and executed at the Tower of London in 1941 - during World War II. He wasn’t detained in the White Tower but the chair in which he was executed is on display on the second floor.
