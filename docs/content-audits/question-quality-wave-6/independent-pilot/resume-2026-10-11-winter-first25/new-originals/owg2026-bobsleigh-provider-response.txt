L9@P0: Olympic Competition Format
L10@P0: There are four bobsleigh events at the Olympic Winter Games Milano Cortina 2026:
L11@P0: 2-man: • Sled minimum weight: 170kg/375lbs
L12@P0: • Athletes and equipment maximum weight: 390.5kg/860lbs
L13@P0: 4-man: • Sled minimum weight: 210kg/463lbs
L14@P0: • Athletes and equipment maximum weight: 631kg/1392lbs
L15@P0: 2-woman: • Sled minimum weight: 170kg/375lbs
L16@P0: • Athletes and equipment maximum weight: 330.5kg/729lbs
L17@P0: Women's monobob: • Sled minimum weight: 163kg/360lbs
L18@P0: • Athlete and equipment maximum weight: 248.5kg/548lbs
L19@P0: All events are held over four descents called "heats" on two consecutive days, with two heats per day. Results are
L20@P0: calculated by adding the times of all competition heats together, with the lowest aggregate time winning. Only the top 20
L21@P0: sleds will take part in the decisive fourth heat.
L22@P0: At least one heat must be conducted for the race to be officially valid. One to three of the four heats may be cancelled due
L23@P0: to weather conditions. At least six official training heats for each of the four events must be offered. Pilots must have
L24@P0: completed at least two valid training heats to qualify for the competition. Entries are limited following a quota-system per
L25@P0: National Olympic Committee (NOC).
L26@P0: Differences between the IBSF World Championships and the Olympic Winter Games
L27@P0: There are no differences in competition format between the IBSF World Championships and the Olympic Winter Games, but
L28@P0: at the IBSF World Championships the qualification criteria are different, resulting in more participating sleds and, under
L29@P0: certain conditions, the top three placed athletes at the Junior World Championships receive an extra wild card.

L31@P0: Women’s Monobob: after successful debut at Beijing 2022, Milano Cortina 2026 will feature 25% increase in female athletes
L32@P0: participating (increasing from 20 to 25).
L33@P0: 2-man Bobsleigh: The field of participants was decreased from 30 to 28.

L48@P1: As of FRI 30 JAN 2026
L49@P1: Bobsleigh teams consist of a pilot in the Women's monobob event, a pilot and a brakewoman/brakeman in the two-man and
L50@P1: two-woman event, and of a pilot, two push athletes and a brakeman in the four-man event.
L51@P1: Race Procedure
L52@P1: Once the crew has loaded into the sled after the start, the pilot steers through twisting, high-speed turns and straights where
L53@P1: top speeds can reach over 140km/h. Pull too hard on the steering ropes and the sled will skid, losing valuable time. Steer
L54@P1: too little and the sled is at the mercy of the track, causing slow times at best, and a crash at worst. Pilots often talk of the
L55@P1: "feel" they need to race well and they develop the sense of when to let the sled run (e.g. G-force is your friend) and when to
L56@P1: steer. Just as downhill skiers try different lines to find the quickest way down a slope, bobsleigh pilots study every twist and
L57@P1: turn on a track to figure out the fastest way to the finish line.
L58@P1: Starting Order:
L59@P1: For Official Training
L60@P1: Official training days are vital - no ice track is ever the same, so over three intense days track familiarisation, technique and
L61@P1: performance are sharpened ready for race day.
L62@P1: The draw dictates the starting order of sleds for training and is organised at the team captains' meeting. The draw is for the
L63@P1: starting order during the first two training days. The starting order of sleds for the third training day is determined based on
L64@P1: the IBSF discipline ranking.
L65@P1: The draw determines the starting order of the NOCs. Sleds within each NOC start in the order in which they are listed in the
L66@P1: entry list submitted by the team captains before the draw.
L67@P1: On the first training day, sleds start in the order drawn in both heats. On the second training day, they start in reverse order
L68@P1: of the draw. The order of sleds within each NOC is the same on both days.
L69@P1: On the third training day, sleds start in the order of the IBSF discipline ranking.
L70@P1: For Competition
L71@P1: The 10 best placed pilots in the IBSF discipline ranking will choose their starting numbers from 1 to 10. All other pilots start
L72@P1: according to the IBSF discipline ranking from starting number 11 onwards.
L73@P1: The 25 best ranked sleds from heat one will start in reverse order for heat two, followed by the remaining sleds (26
L74@P1: onwards). For the third heat, sleds will start in the ranking order after heats one and two (i.e. fastest first), and in the last
L75@P1: heat the 20 best ranked sleds will start in reverse order.
L76@P1: Timing and Scoring
L77@P1: Timing and Scoring is provided by OMEGA in accordance with IBSF rules and Olympic Results and Information Services
L78@P1: (ORIS). Two independent timing systems, each consisting of at least one time measuring device, complete set of light
L79@P1: beams and independent power supply, must be available and in operation during a competition (A and B systems).
L80@P1: The A system comprises all light beams for start, start time, four intermediate times, four speeds and finish time. The first
L81@P1: interval time corresponds to the start time (50 m). Four interval times must be evenly distributed among the remainder of the
L82@P1: track. The time of day of both timing systems (A and B systems) must be synchronised and must provide accurate timing
L83@P1: to1/100th of a second.
L84@P1: Team Changes
L85@P1: During the competition pilots cannot be changed but, for medical reasons and under certain conditions, change of a
L86@P1: brakewoman/brakeman or push athlete is possible in the case of injury or illness.
L87@P1: Forerunners
L88@P1: The Jury determines the number of forerunners (also referred to as pilot sleds). The organiser must provide a minimum of
L89@P1: five forerunners.

L99@P2: As of FRI 30 JAN 2026
L100@P2: Sleds sharing the same aggregate time are tied and share the same rank. For example: at the Olympic Winter Games
L101@P2: PyeongChang 2018 two gold medals were awarded in two-man and no silver; two bronze medals were awarded in four-man
L102@P2: during the Olympic Winter Games Nagano 1998.
L103@P2: Penalties / Disqualification Rules
L104@P2: During the heats, sleds may be disqualified for various reasons. The most common ones are: overweight sled, runner
L105@P2: temperature higher than allowed, incomplete team at the finish and late start.
L106@P2: All medal-winning sleds are checked immediately after the final heat. Upon the findings and recommendations of the
L107@P2: materials commission, the jury may disqualify a sled after the end of a competition.
L108@P2: Protests / Appeals
L109@P2: Protests must be made to the jury. The rules state that verbal announcement of the protest must be made within five
L110@P2: minutes after the end of the heat in which the incident occurred. Additionally, the grounds for the protest must be submitted
L111@P2: in writing to the jury no more than 20 minutes after the conclusion of the race.
