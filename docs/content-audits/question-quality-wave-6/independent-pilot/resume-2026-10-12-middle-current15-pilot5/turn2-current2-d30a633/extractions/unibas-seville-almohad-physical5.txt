In addition to the above-mentioned features, 
mention can be made of the following:
- Dense orchards concentrated in the north in 
an area rich in subterranean water.
- The development of orchards in connection 
with the aqueduct channel, and also the 
construction of watermills.
- The existence of areas dedicated to pastures 
opposite the city walls. This was the case of 
the Prado de Santa Justa and the Prado de 
San Sebastian, both meadows on the east-
ern side, and, finally, a grassland called de- 
hesa de Tablada to the south. These three 
pasture areas were a gift from King Fernan-
do III to the city council of Seville after the 
Christian conquest.
- Three districts are known on the outskirts, 
namely Triana, Macarena and San Benialo- 
far (nowadays San Bernardo).
The existence of towers 6.5 or 7 kilometres 
away from the city walls has been detected. 
This is the case (from north to south) of torre de 
Cuartos, Quiontos, Torreblanca, torre del Judio, 
Tercia, and one in the west (torre de Cam- 
bogaz, 1.5 kilometres in length). These towers 
probably define the municipality of Seville.
