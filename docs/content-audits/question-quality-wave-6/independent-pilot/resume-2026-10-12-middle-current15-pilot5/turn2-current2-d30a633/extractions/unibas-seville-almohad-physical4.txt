Markets, baths and houses
Nowadays, only one of the market structures 
founded by the Almohads can be seen. This 
is the case of the Alcaicerfa (market for luxury 
items) created opposite the main gate of the 
courtyard's Friday mosque. It was a 65 x 55 metre 
(3,575 square metre) area, formed by a princi-
pal axis (Hernando Colon Street), intersected 
by two narrower streets.
Preserved remains of five baths still exist, al-
though only the 'hot' and 'warm' rooms sur-
vive. One of them, Banos de la Reina Mora, 
has been excavated several times since 1984. 
It dates back to Almohad times owing to its ar-
chaeological context and the architectural ele-
ments, namely capitels and brick decoration 
on vaults.
Concerning residences, some information is 
now known thanks to archaeological excava-
tion. The city layout which still exists in the his-
toric centre was formed during this period. 
Houses seemed to be very large, with court- 
yards used as gardens and orchards. Some of 
them were shaped like crosses, with pools or 
small-scale irrigation systems, including a well, 
waterwheel, irrigation channel and smallhold-
ings. Rooms were on two sides, normally north 
and south, and they were very long with one 
small separation at the end (alhanias).
The building material tended to be re-used: 
stones, bricks and tapial. These walls were 
covered with plaster and frequently painted in 
dark-red (almagra) or mustard-yellow (calamo- 
cha). Pavements could be of dark-red earth, 
lime or bricks.
The outskirts
This aspect is still fairly unknown and has 
never been studied systematically. The outskirts 
of Isbiliya were re-organised at the same time as 
the medina, namely:
- In 1171 the caliph Abu Yaqub ordered the 
construction of a boat-bridge over the Gua-
dalquivir river. At this period, several bridg-
es over the Tagarete river were also built. 
- One year later, the re-construction of a 17 kil-
ometre long aqueduct was begun. Ten kilo-
metres were underground, 5.3 kilometres 
were a canal and only the last 1.7 kilometres 
formed an architectural structure consisting 
of arches.
- Palaces built on the outskirts by the Almohad 
caliphs were known as almunias.
The two known cases are:
Al-Buhayra, constructed by Abu Yaqub in 
1172, to the east of the medina. A large pool 
(43 x 43 metres) is preserved as the centre of 
an irrigation system. A huge orchard serving 
as a botanical garden was created there. The 
written sources indicate that the soil was 
changed, and the best fruit trees from the 
whole of al-Andalus were planted there. The 
only surviving Islamic building is a small pavil-
ion (6.77 x 6.77 metres) to the south of the 
pool.

west of Seville. From this vantage point, the river 
Guadalquivir and the city are dominated. The
