The following examples of the preserved forti-
fication can be mentioned:
A) Bab Yahwar wall (located between the 
Yahwar gate and the urban caste, or alcazar).
B) Walls from the river side. This area was re-
built twice in this period, firstly in 1168/1169 
and secondly in 1201; on both occasions re-
building was the consequence of a violent 
flood.
In 1998, an archaeological excavation opposite 
the Macarena gate showed the fore-mentioned 
walls with a 2-metre separation.
The most significant factor of this expansion to 
the north and west of the city walls was the 
conquest of a space which was commonly oc-
cupied by the river, as well as the beginning of 
a drainage process.
C) There were twelve gates, but only two are 
still preserved in their original style, namely 
the Córdoba gate and the postigo (postern) del 
Aceite. The former is a tower with an elbow 
entrance, while the latter is a gate flanked by 
two towers.
D) Wall between Macarena and Córdoba gates. 
A 536 metre perimeter from serpentine city 
walls, formed by eight towers and two gates 
with an outer wall.
Two phases can be detected: the Almohad 
wall (1168/1169 or 1201) and the outer wall 
of 1220/1221. The wall, however, needed to 
be higher and three layers of adobe (2.4 me-
tres in total) were added.
The urban castle (alcazar and alcazabas)
After the second half of the 12th century, the 
south of the city was consolidated as a political 
centre. To the enclosures built by the Omey- 
ads and Petty kings, the Almohads added 
many more, which are known as alcazabas in 
the written sources. These new enclosures had 
different functions, as garden, orchard, and 
cemetery, or as a fort (in the area opposite the 
river, because of their exceptional walls), and 
as industrial-state monopolies and residences. 
Archaeological research in this area has only 
been possible recently since 1997. It is now 
known that the pre-Almohad palaces were 
systematically demolished, and the new ones 
were built with a different orientation, para-
meters and aesthetics.
Common characteristics in Seville's 
fortifications are:
A. Building material. It was tapial, which is a mix-
ture of pebbles in cement, sun-dried between 
wooden boards. Different kinds of soil can be 
discerned in such a log perimeter, and, conse-
quently, different qualities of tapial. The wooden 
boards were 0.8 metres high and of variable 
length, with a maximum of 2.5 metres. Bricks 
were used in vaults and openings.
B. Typology. Towers were spaced out at regular 
intervals. Most of them are square, and only ex-
ceptionally do they have different forms, such as 
hexagons (torre de Abd al-Aziz), octagons (torre 
Blanca, torre de la Plata) and dodecagons (torre 
del Oro). Vaults are always the same: shaft, arris 
or groined. Another common feature is exterior 
decoration formed with a double line of bricks 
located in specific places, such as the chamber 
pavement or surrounding the arrow slits.

example, after the Christian conquest, the

palaces and created new ones. This is the case

second half of the 13th century) and the pala-
