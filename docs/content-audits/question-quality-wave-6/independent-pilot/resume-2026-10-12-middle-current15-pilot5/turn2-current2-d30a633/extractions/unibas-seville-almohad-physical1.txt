Seville as Capital of the Almohad Empire in 
Al-Andalus
Magdalena Valor Piechotta

Hispalis was under Muslim rule for five and 
a half centuries; its Arabic name was Isbiliya. 
This lengthy time can be divided into two peri-
ods, namely:
1) 711 or 712 (the Muslim Conquest) to 1147 
(Almohad occupation)
2) 1147 to 1248 (Christian conquest)
These periods can be re-constructed thanks to 
the written sources, historiography, iconogra-
phy and archaeological research.
Little is known about the first period, as the 
written sources offer scarce information, and 
only a few remains are preserved. If we add to 
this the scarcity of archaeological research, it 
becomes clear that specific research is neces-
sary in order to fill these gaps.
Isbiliya from 1147 to 1248
Both the chronicles and the evidence we have 
indicate that the Almohad caliphs were great 
builders. They constructed urban fortifications, 
places, water supply systems (aqueducts), brid-
ges, docks, shipyards, mosques and castles; not 
only in the Magreb, but also in al-Andalus.
Isbiliya, as capital of the Almohads in al-Anda-
lus, was converted into a metropolis. The city 
was completely transformed, especially be-
tween 1156 and 1199. Therefore, Seville 
would seem to be a good example of urban 
development in the High Middle Ages.
Thanks to the written sources, the formation of 
the metropolis can be described as follows:
The city
1. New urban fortification. Specific areas men-
tioned in chronicles were the riverside walls 
(west side) and the Bab Yahwar wall (the con-
nection between the city walls and the urban 
castle, or alcazar).
2. The construction of new enclosures around 
the pre-Almohad royal residence or alcazar, 
named alcazabas.
3. A new Friday mosque, built in a residential 
area to the south. A huge mosque of the di-
mensions of the one found in Córdoba.
4. The creation of a new bazaar area, reserved 
for luxury items (alcaiceria). This is a group of 
streets, accessible only through four gates and 
closed at night.
The outskirts
1. Several bridges were constructed. A boat 
bridge on the Guadalquivir and stone bridges 
on the Tagarete, opposite the gates (puertas de 
Jérez, Carne, Carmona and Córdoba).
2. Royal summer residences, al-Buhayra and 
hisn al-Faray.
3. The re-construction of an abandoned aque-
duct named Canos de Carmona.
The city
Urban fortification
The city walls seen today in Seville were con-
structed in the period between 1147 and 
1248. As a consequence, very few remains of 
pre-Almohad walls are recognisable in the Al-
cazar area. The enclosure had a 7,000 metre 
perimeter, but no more than 2,000 metres can 
be seen nowadays. The 273 hectare surface of 
the city was much more than was necessary.
