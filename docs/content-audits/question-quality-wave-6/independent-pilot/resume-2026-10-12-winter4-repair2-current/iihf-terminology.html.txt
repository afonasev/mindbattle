NODE 14: Ice Hockey Terminology
NODE 15: Term
NODE 16: Definition
NODE 17: Alternate Captain
NODE 18: A maximum of two skaters per team can be designated by their Head Coach as Alternate Captains. They are identified with an “A” on front top left of their jersey. When the Captain is not on the ice an Alternate Captain who is on the ice can act as Captain.
NODE 19: Assist
NODE 20: Awarded to the offensive player(s) who control the puck immediately preceding control by the goal scorer. A maximum of two assists can be credited for one goal. There is no minimum.
NODE 21: Assistant Coach
NODE 22: A member of the playing team’s staff that is positioned on the player’s bench and aides the Head Coach in managing the players and team tactics.
NODE 23: Attacking Zone
NODE 24: A member of the playing team’s staff that is positioned on the player’s bench and aides the Head Coach in managing the players and team tactics.
NODE 25: Backcheck
NODE 26: Attempt by a player, skating back to their defensive zone, to regain the puck from the opposition by checking or harassing an opponent who has the puck.
NODE 27: Backhand
NODE 28: Shot or pass made from the non-curved side of the stick.
NODE 29: Back Pass
NODE 30: Pass in the direction of a player's own goal while the team is moving forward, usually to maintain puck control.
NODE 31: Blind Pass
NODE 32: To pass the puck without looking at the intended recipient.
NODE 33: Blocker
NODE 34: Goalkeeper equipment worn on the hand holding the stick, used to block shots on that side.
NODE 35: Blue Lines
NODE 36: Two blue, 30cm wide lines running parallel across the ice. They divide the rink into three zones called the attacking, defending and neutral (or centre) zones. The defending blue line is the closer to a player's own net, while the attacking blue line is the one farther from a player's net and is used in determining offside.
NODE 37: Boards or Wall
NODE 38: Fibre glass or composite approximately 1.2m high, which encloses the playing surface; all rinks have shatterproof glass that rises above the boards to provide additional protection.
NODE 39: Body Check
NODE 40: A legal body check is one in which a skater checks an opponent who is in possession of the puck, by using their hip or body from the front, diagonally from the front or straight from the side. Legitimate body checking must be done for the purpose of separating the opponent from the puck, only with the trunk of the body (hips and shoulders) and must be above the opponent’s knees and at or below the shoulders.
NODE 41: Breakaway
NODE 42: Fast break in which an attacker with the puck skates in alone on the opposing goalkeeper, having skated past or clear of all other opponents.
NODE 43: Captain
NODE 44: Player representing the team who is responsible for discussions with the officials. They are identified with a “C” on the front top left of their jersey.
NODE 45: Catching Glove (Catcher)
NODE 46: Special goalkeeper's glove worn on the non-stick hand, which is intended to catch or stop shots.
NODE 47: Centre
NODE 48: The central player on the forward line who usually leads the team's attack when it is trying to score a goal. This player also takes part in most of the face-offs for their team.
NODE 49: Centre Ice Spot and Circle
NODE 50: Circle, measuring 9.1m in diameter, at the centre of the ice, where the puck is dropped in a face-off to start each period of play and to restart the game after a goal has been scored.
NODE 51: Centre Ice
NODE 52: Area between the two blue lines, also called the neutral zone.
NODE 53: Centre Line
NODE 54: Red, 30cm wide line across the ice, midway between the two goals, which is used primarily to determine icing calls.
NODE 55: Check or Checking
NODE 56: Legal contact initiated by a defending player against a puck-carrying opponent to prevent any advance towards the goal. There are two main types of checks: stick checks and body checks. These are allowed only against a player in control of the puck or against the last player to control it immediately after giving it up. Checking after too many steps or strides becomes charging.
NODE 57: Clearing the Puck
NODE 58: Getting the puck out of one's own defensive zone.
NODE 59: Clearing the Zone
NODE 60: When the puck comes outside the blue line, all the attacking players must also skate outside the blue line to avoid being called offside when the puck re-enters the zone.
NODE 61: Combined Ranking
NODE 62: After the completion of all the games of the preliminary round of the men's tournament, teams from the groups A, B and C will be merged into a new group D based on their preliminary round results (ranked 1D - 12D).
NODE 63: Crease
NODE 64: Semi-circular area on ice (painted blue) with a 1.82m radius in front of each goal. Players are allowed to pass through but not establish position in this area designated to protect goalkeepers and allow them a fair opportunity to stop the puck.
NODE 65: Cross Bar
NODE 66: Horizontal bar connecting the tops of the two goal posts, 1.22m above the ice surface.
NODE 67: Dead Puck
NODE 68: Puck that flies out of the rink or that is no longer in play, forcing a stoppage in play.
NODE 69: Deke or Deking
NODE 70: When the puck carrier feigns a move to make an opponent shift in one direction while the puck carrier then quickly moves the opposite way.
NODE 71: Defenders
NODE 72: Two of the six players of one team on the ice whose priority in the defensive end is to stay close to the goalkeeper and help prevent goals.
NODE 73: Defensive Zone
NODE 74: The area inside a team's own blue line.
NODE 75: Deflection
NODE 76: A pass or shot that is redirected intentionally or otherwise.
NODE 77: Delayed Penalty
NODE 78: Penalty that cannot be immediately served due to prior penalties currently being served by the penalised team having reduce their playing strength to three skaters and a goalkeeper. The penalty starts when a penalty expires that would have otherwise resulted in a playing strength of four skaters and a goalkeeper.
NODE 79: Delayed Whistle or
NODE 80: Delayed Call
NODE 81: When a referee indicates a penalty but does not blow the whistle because the non-offending team is still in possession of the puck. Only when the offending team gains control of the puck does the referee stop play to assess the penalty.
NODE 82: Delay of the Game
NODE 83: Minor penalty imposed on any player who purposely delays the game in any way, such as shooting or batting the puck outside the playing area or displacing a goalpost.
NODE 84: Directorate
NODE 85: The Directorate is an executive body of the IIHF composed of the tournament chairperson and a representative of each playing team. The Directorate is charged ensuring the integrity of the tournament or competition. Separate Directorates exist for the Men's and Women's tournaments.
NODE 86: Double Minor
NODE 87: Two concurrent minor penalties assessed to the same player.
NODE 88: Drop Pass
NODE 89: When the puck carrier leaves the puck for a crossing or trailing teammate to pick up, either for an immediate shot or to further advance the attack.
NODE 90: Empty-Net Goal
NODE 91: Goal scored against a team that has pulled the goalkeeper.
NODE 92: End Boards
NODE 93: Boards at each end of the rink.
NODE 94: Face Mask
NODE 95: Protective mask worn by the goalkeeper in Men's or Women's play or by players in the Women's competition.
NODE 96: Face-Off
NODE 97: Method of starting play when the official drops the puck between the sticks of two opposing players standing one stick-length apart with stick blades flat on the ice. It is used to begin each period and to resume play after a whistle during periods.
NODE 98: Face-Off Circles and Spots
NODE 99: Various circular spots on the ice where an official and two players hold a face-off to resume play. There is one blue face-off circle and four red face-off spots located in the neutral zone. Two red face-off circles are located at each end of the ice.
NODE 100: Field of Play
NODE 101: Includes the ice surface, players' benches, scorekeeper's bench, penalty benches, all boards and glass.
NODE 102: Five Hole
NODE 103: Space in between the goalkeeper's legs. Derived from target practice sheets used to cover the mouth of the ice hockey goal, which have a hole in each of the four corners of the goal. A fifth hole is where the space between the goalkeeper's legs would be.
NODE 104: Forecheck
NODE 105: To check or harass an opponent who has the puck in the offensive zone, the purpose of which is to gain control and create an opportunity to score a goal.
NODE 106: Forehand
NODE 107: A shot or pass taken from the curved side of the stick.
NODE 108: Forward Line
NODE 109: Consists of two wings (right and left) and a centre. These three players play nearer the opponent's goal and are responsible for most of the scoring. Teams generally employ four forward lines during a game on a rotating basis, at the coach's discretion.
NODE 110: Forwards
NODE 111: The three players who make up the attacking line of a team: The centre, and the right and left wings.
NODE 112: Freeze the Puck
NODE 113: By a player: To hold the puck against the boards with the skate or stick in an effort to stop play.
NODE 114: By a goalkeeper: To make a save and hold the puck long enough to stop play.
NODE 115: Full Strength
NODE 116: When a team has a full complement of six players on the ice.
NODE 117: Game
NODE 118: A game is a competition between two teams that comprises three twenty (20) minute stop time periods, with an overtime period and the Shootout procedure if required.
NODE 119: Goal
NODE 120: A shot attempt during play that completely crosses the goal line results in a score for the attacking team.
NODE 121: Goal Cage
NODE 122: A 1.82m wide by 1.22m high tubular steel frame consisting of a cross bar and two goalposts to which a net is attached.
NODE 123: Goal Crease
NODE 124: See "Crease".
NODE 125: Goal Line
NODE 126: A 5cm wide red line between the goalposts that stretches in both directions to the sideboards.
NODE 127: Goalkeeper
NODE 128: The heavily padded player who guards the goal and attempts to prevent opponents from scoring by stopping the puck any way possible.
NODE 129: Sometimes referred to as a goaltender or goalie.
NODE 130: Goalposts
NODE 131: The vertical metal bars that frame the area to which the net is attached, which rests on the centre of the goal line, and between which a puck must completely pass to score a goal.
NODE 132: Group
NODE 133: In the preliminary round teams will be seeded into two groups for women and three groups for men. Each group will consist of four teams. Within a group each team plays one game against every other team in the group.
NODE 134: Hat Trick
NODE 135: Three goals scored by a player in one game.
NODE 136: High Stick
NODE 137: Batting the puck while the stick is above the height of the crossbar, resulting in a whistle if the offending team touches the puck or resulting in the nullification of a goal if the batted puck enters the net.
NODE 138: Ice Resurfacer
NODE 139: A large vehicle used to clean and smooth the surface of an ice rink. Also colloquially referred to as a 'Zamboni', although this is a brand name.
NODE 140: Icing
NODE 141: A violation that occurs when the team in possession of the puck shoots it from behind the red centre line across the opponent's goal line (but not into the goal) without a player from either side touching the puck. If a member of the opposing team touches it first, play will be halted and a face-off will occur in the offender's defensive zone. If the attacking team touches the puck first or the officials deem the defending team had reasonable opportunity to play the puck, no icing will be called. A shorthanded team cannot be called for icing.
NODE 142: Intermission
NODE 143: A fifteen (15) minute recess between each of the three periods of an Ice Hockey game.
NODE 144: Jersey
NODE 145: The top part of the uniform of the player worn over the protective equipment which indicates to which team the player belongs.
NODE 146: Lane
NODE 147: A specific section of ice in which no opponent can be found and to which players are encouraged to skate to receive a pass or skate unobstructed.
NODE 148: Line Change
NODE 149: The substitution of any or all players either as play continues or during a stoppage in play, usually involving the three forwards.
NODE 150: Linespersons
NODE 151: The on-ice game officials assisting the referees in their duties on the ice. There is one linesperson toward each end of the rink, responsible for monitoring infractions of the rules concerning offside plays at the blue lines and for any icing violations. They conduct most of the face-offs, sometimes advise the referee concerning penalties, and separate players who are fighting.
NODE 152: National Hockey League
NODE 153: (NHL)
NODE 154: Professional league founded in, 1917, which currently includes 32 teams, 25 of which are based in the United States and seven in Canada.
NODE 155: Neutral Zone
NODE 156: Area between the two blue lines.
NODE 157: Off-Ice Officials
NODE 158: The officials who assist the on-ice officials in conducting a game. They comprise the game and referee supervisors, the goal judges, the scorekeeper, the scorekeeper assistants, the timekeeper, the announcer and the penalty box attendants.
NODE 159: On-Ice Officials
NODE 160: The assigned game officials responsible for the conduct of the game in accordance with the official rules and for maintaining discipline and order on the ice during the game. They are the Referee and Linespersons.
NODE 161: Offside
NODE 162: A violation that occurs when both skates of an attacking player cross the plane of the opponent's blue line before the puck is passed or carried into the attacking zone. Also called when a player passes the puck from his or her defending zone to a teammate across the far blue line.
NODE 163: Olympia
NODE 164: A brand name of an ice resurfacer.
NODE 165: One-timer
NODE 166: An immediate shot off a teammate's pass without first stopping or controlling the puck.
NODE 167: Overtime
NODE 168: If a game is tied at the conclusion time a period of overtime will take place.
NODE 169: The length of an overtime period is:
NODE 170: five (5) minutes for the preliminary round games,
NODE 171: ten (10) minutes for play-off games except the Gold medal game,
NODE 172: twenty (20) minutes for the Gold medal game.
NODE 173: The overtime period will stop after the first goal is scored ("sudden death").
NODE 174: Penalty
NODE 175: An infraction against ice hockey rules that may result in the offending player (or a player from the offending team) being required to serve time in a penalty box off the ice, or the other team being allowed to attempt a penalty shot. The time served in the penalty box can range from two (2) minutes to the balance of the game. Penalties often result in inferior playing strength during the penalty.
NODE 176: Penalties are divided into the following categories showing the time to be served:
NODE 177: Minor penalty (two (2) minutes)
NODE 178: Double minor penalty (four (4) minutes)
NODE 179: Bench minor penalty (two (2) minutes)
NODE 180: Major penalty (five (5) minutes)
NODE 181: Misconduct penalty (ten (10) minutes)
NODE 182: Game misconduct penalty (balance of the game, with substitution allowed immediately)
NODE 183: A more detailed description of the different penalties can be found in the output "Game, Equipment, Penalty Description" (N02C).
NODE 184: Penalty Box
NODE 185: Boxes located near centre ice on the side opposite to the players' benches (one for each team) where penalised players must remain until their penalty time has expired or until a power-play goal is scored (excepting game misconduct penalties, for which a player is barred from the playing area immediately).
NODE 186: Penalty Killing
NODE 187: The team that is short of one or two players due to penalties is engaged in penalty killing. Strategies to wait out the penalty time safely include shooting the puck down the length of the ice or making a "box" formation (four players), or "triangle" formation (three players) inside its own blue line to try to defend against a superior number of opposing players.
NODE 188: Penalty Shot
NODE 189: An undefended shot awarded to a team if: (a) a player is fouled while on a clear break in on goal; (b) a defending player other than the goalkeeper covers the puck with a glove in the crease; (c) a defending player intentionally knocks the goal off its moorings with less than two (2) minutes left in a game.
NODE 190: Period
NODE 191: A sixty (60) minute game consists of three periods of twenty (20) minutes (regulation time) plus overtime period(s) if required.
NODE 192: PIM
NODE 193: Abbreviation used to indicate a player's total penalties in minutes.
NODE 194: Players
NODE 195: Skaters and goalkeepers
NODE 196: Plus/ Minus
NODE 197: Individual player statistic accounting for goals scored for and against a player's team while the player is on the ice and the teams are at equal playing strength. When a goal is scored for a player's team while a player is on the ice each player is credited with a plus, is counted as +1. If a goal is scored against a player's team while the player is on the ice each player is credited with a minus, and it is counted as -1. The total for the player is known as plus/minus. Team goals accrued during the power play does not count toward this statistic, and plus/minus is not calculated for goalkeepers.
NODE 198: Puck
NODE 199: Black, vulcanised rubber disc, 2.5cm thick and 7.6cm in diameter, weighing between 155g and 170g used to play Ice Hockey. Pucks are frozen to prevent excessive bouncing and improve handling, and are changed throughout the game.
NODE 200: Pulling the Goalkeeper
NODE 201: Tactic that may be employed by the trailing team that involves removing the goalkeeper from the ice in favour of an extra skater. This tactic leaves the goal unguarded so is only used as a attempt to score in the closing minutes of a game. Also used during a "Delayed Whistle" or "Delayed Call Penalty" by the non-offending team to increase the odds of scoring, safe in the knowledge that a referee will blow the whistle as soon as the offending team touches the puck.
NODE 202: Rebound
NODE 203: Shot that bounces off the goalkeeper's body or equipment, free to be claimed by the offensive team again.
NODE 204: Referee
NODE 205: The on-ice game official responsible for the conduct of the competition in accordance with the official IIHF rules and for maintaining discipline and order on the playing field during the game.
NODE 206: Regulation Time
NODE 207: Three periods of twenty (20) minutes. Regulation time does not include overtime or Shootout phases.
NODE 208: Roster
NODE 209: List of players on a team for a particular game or tournament.
NODE 210: Round
NODE 211: This is the progression system in competition. Ice Hockey consists of the following rounds: Preliminary round and Play-off round.
NODE 212: Save
NODE 213: The act of a goalkeeper blocking or stopping a shot that would otherwise have been a goal.
NODE 214: Screen Shot
NODE 215: Shot on goal that the goalkeeper cannot see because an attacking or defending player obscured their vision by standing directly in the line of sight between the goalkeeper and the shooter.
NODE 216: Shift
NODE 217: Time a player is on the ice between breaks on the bench. Most shifts last fifty (50) seconds or less.
NODE 218: Shootout
NODE 219: If a game remains tied at the conclusion of the overtime period, it will be decided by a Shootout.
NODE 220: Shorthanded
NODE 221: When a team is short of one or more players due to penalties and the opposing team has superior player advantage. (five-on-four, five-on-three, or four-on-three are the most common such situations). This situation is a power play for the other team.
NODE 222: Shot on Goal (SOG)
NODE 223: A shot that either goes into the net, or would have gone into the net had the goalkeeper not made a save. Hitting the post or crossbar does not count as a shot on goal.
NODE 224: Skaters
NODE 225: Forward(s) and/or defender(s)
NODE 226: Slap Shot
NODE 227: Shot in which the player raises the stick in a backswing to approximately waist height prior to striking the puck in a single motion, usually as an attempted Shot on Goal. A slap shot can reach speeds of up to 175 km/h but is less accurate than a wrist shot.
NODE 228: Speed
NODE 229: Player or team attacking quickness that limits the reaction time of the defence and may force defensive errors.
NODE 230: Stick
NODE 231: Held by skaters and used to pass or carry the puck. The stick has a straight shaft held by the players and a curved blade for carrying, passing, or shooting the puck. The blade is made up of a heel (area closest to the shaft) and a toe (area furthest from the shaft). A goalkeeper’s stick has a wider blade and shaft and is used primarily for defending the goal.
NODE 232: Substitution
NODE 233: A player on the bench entering the field of play to replace a player who is leaving the field of play. Substitutions can be made at any time and play does not need to stop.
NODE 234: Sudden Death
NODE 235: The time during overtime or during a Shootout after 10 shots have been taken when a decisive goal will immediately end the game.
NODE 236: Third Player-In Rule
NODE 237: Rule stating that at the discretion of the referee, the first player to intervene in an altercation (third player in) is assessed a game misconduct penalty. This penalty is typically only applied during fights.
NODE 238: Transition
NODE 239: When a team to quickly changes tactics from offence to defence and vice versa, usually preceding by a change in puck possession.
NODE 240: Waved off
NODE 241: A signal by a game official to indicate an attempted goal did not completely cross the goal line, or that a potential offside was in fact onside.
NODE 242: Wings
NODE 243: Two players who flank the centre on the right and left sides. The threesome makes up a forward line.
NODE 244: Wraparound Goal
NODE 245: When an offensive player carries the puck around the back of the net from one side and attempts to score by quickly pushing the puck into the net between the goalkeeper and the far post of the net.
NODE 246: Wrist Shot
NODE 247: Shot using a strong flicking of the wrist and forearm muscles, with the stick blade kept on the ice. The puck travels slower than a slapshot but is more accurately shot.
NODE 248: Zones
NODE 249: Three areas defined by the two blue lines. The attacking zone is the area inside the blue line farthest from the goal a player is defending. The neutral zone is the central area between the blue lines. The defending zone is the area inside the blue line where a player's goal is and where the team's goalkeeper is positioned.
NODE 250: IIHF is committed to protecting everyone involved within ice hockey.
