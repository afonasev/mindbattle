NODE 707: PROSHARP FAQS
NODE 708: Why do you need to sharpen your skates?
NODE 709: You need two sharp edges when skating on ice. These edges are called inner- and outer edges. It's the combination of having a sharp inner edge which the skater pushes off from and transfer that force onto the other skate's both edges which the skater glides on. Then the same process is repeated when the glide phase slows down and it goes into pushing off again.
NODE 710: The edges gets worn down and/or damaged when skating. New skates do not have any edges from the start. A simple rule is that dull or no edges makes you fall on your ass a lot.
NODE 711: To maintain the edges or make new edges you need to sharpen them with a grinding wheel.
NODE 712: What is the Radius of Hollow?
NODE 713: The curve of the grinding wheel when seen in a cross section decides what radius of hollow you get on your blades.
NODE 714: By reshaping the stone grinding wheels you can change the curve and subsequently the radius of hollow (see example picture and video below).
NODE 715: Or by changing the electroplated wheels, this applies for SkatePal (100 mm, 60 mm) and Prosharp Home (60 mm). The EP wheels has a fixed radius of hollow and thes can not be dressed (see example picture and video below).
NODE 716: A deep radius of hollow gives you more grip and less glide. This is because the deeper hollow generates more friction and that makes you dig into the ice more.
NODE 717: A flatter radius of hollow gives less grip but more glide. This is because the flatter hollow digs less into the ice and it leads to less friction. Making you float on top of the ice more.
NODE 718: The most ideal radius of hollow , according to us, is to reduce the radius of hollow as much as possible and still maintain the grip by choosing the correct profile. Reducing the radius of hollow will also lead to less stress on the skater's body and help the skater reserve stamina.
NODE 719: Radius of hollow is stated in either inches (US/Canada/some parts of Europe) or millimetres (Europe).
NODE 720: What is a profile?
NODE 721: A profile is the shape of the blade when seen from the side (or in profile). Prosharp Profiling® has been under development since the first machine hit the market in 1986 and it can help and maximize skaters ability. Prosharp Profiling® focuses on four areas; acceleration, agility, speed and stability. By choosing the correct profile you can get help or maximize acceleration, agility, speed and stability. Choosing the correct profile combined with a shallower hollow can also put less stress onto hips, groins and knees, leading to less injuries. What's the correct profile for each skater is very individual and it all boils down to the feeling on the ice.
NODE 722: Radius of profile is stated in either feet (most common) or metres. With combination profiles this means that the combination is stated from the toe to the heel. If you have a Zuperior Medium your combination is 6 ft – 13 ft – 20 ft.
NODE 723: What hollow is the best for you?
NODE 724: To get a general recommendation for choosing the radius of hollow you can use this formula: bodyweight/3,14 (Pi). Example 80 kg/3,14=25,47, round off to 25. There you have a radius of hollow to start with.
NODE 725: What profile is the best for you?
NODE 726: When being a beginner skater we recommend that the beginner gets as much ice contact as possible. This is to provide stability to the skater. Good beginner profiles are 17 ft.- or 19 ft single profiles.
NODE 727: When the skater has reached the next stage in hers or his skating development go down to less ice contact. This is to add more acceleration/agility to the skating.
NODE 728: Depending on personal preference the skater could try anything from 10 ft. to 15 ft.
NODE 729: After that it's time to test combination profiles. Start with the dual profiles, it combines two radii in one profile:
NODE 730: Detroit 1 (10 ft. – 20 ft.) –Combine both good acceleration/agility with great speed/stability.
NODE 731: Detroit 2 (13 ft. – 26 ft.) – A profile focused onmaximum glide speed and especially stability but has enough agility in the front to outmanoeuvre your opponents.
NODE 732: Win 1 (9.5 ft. – 10.5 ft.) – A profile focused on great acceleration and agility.
NODE 733: Win 2 (10 ft. – 11 ft.) – A profile focused on great acceleration and agility.
NODE 734: Combi 10 – 13 (10 ft. – 13 ft.) – A profile focused on great acceleration and agility but also providing good speed and stability.
NODE 735: Combi 9 – 10 (9 ft. – 10 ft.) – A profile focused on excellent acceleration and agility.
NODE 736: Toronto 1 (7 ft. – 10 ft.) – A profile focused on maximum acceleration and great agility.
NODE 737: Toronto 2 (9 ft. – 15 ft.) – A profile focused on excellent acceleration and agility but also providing very good speed and stability.
NODE 738: Toronto 3 (11 ft. – 17 ft.) – A profile focused on great acceleration and agility but also providing excellent speed and stability.
NODE 739: The description is only a general recommendation and the skater needs to test different profiles to find what will suit that specific player's style of skating.
NODE 740: The latest in Prosharp Profiling® is profiles with combinations of three radii and four radii. We call the three radii combination Zuperior and the four radii combination Quad. We recommend the Zuperior profiles to wingers and defensive D-men because it gives great straight line speed with great glide and excellent balance/stability. The Quad profiles are recommended to centers and offensive D-men because the ability to pivot/change direction together with excellent agility and speed.
NODE 741: We have developed different Zuperior- and Quad profiles depending on the skate size:
NODE 742: Skate size 1-2: Zuperior 3XS & Quad 3XS.
NODE 743: Skate size 3-4: Zuperior XXS & Quad XXS.
