L31@P2: Step 2: Resolution, accuracy, and scale
L32@P2: It is important to understand the difference between the accuracy and the resolution of the imagery.
L33@P2: Accuracy tells you how closely the image matches the true value on the ground. Resolution refers to the
L34@P2: measurement of the finest detail that can be displayed.
L35@P2: In addition to accuracy and resolution there is also scale which is shown by a scale bar. Scale bars
L36@P2: provide a visual indication of the size of features and distance between features on the map. A scale bar
L37@P2: is a line or bar divided into parts. The parts are representative of ground length, usually in multiples of
L38@P2: map units. Large scale maps are represented by fractions that are large. Small scale maps are
L39@P2: represented by fractions that are small. A town map is a large-scale map which might be on a scale of
L40@P2: 1:10,000. A world map is a small-scale map which would be on a scale of 1:100,000,000.
L41@P2: 1. Turn off all the layers except the World Imagery Layer.
L42@P2: 2. Expand the World Imagery layer to show its contents.

L55@P4: Step 3: Observing raster data
L56@P4: In its simplest form, a raster consists of a matrix of cells (or pixels) organized into rows and columns (or a
L57@P4: grid) where each cell contains a value representing information, such as elevation, temperature, or land
L58@P4: cover. The World Imagery Layer in the previous section is an example of a raster image. In this section
L59@P4: you will investigate a digital model where each pixel represents an elevation value in meters.
L60@P4: 1. In the navigation bar, click the Home button to zoom to the full extent of the map.
L61@P4: 2. Turn on the World Elevation GMTED layer.
L62@P4: 3. Zoom in until you can see individual pixels of the layer.

L65@P4: Step 4: Examine attributes and legends of vector layers
L66@P4: There are three layers left to examine in your beginning GIS layer map: World Countries, World Cities,
L67@P4: and World Rivers. These are examples of vector layers. Each layer has a spatial component (polygon,
L68@P4: point, line) that is dynamically linked to a table of attributes. Each layer has its own unique table of
L69@P4: attributes that can be accessed by clicking the Show Table button below the layer.
L70@P4: 1. Turn off the World Elevation GMTED and World Imagery layers.
L71@P4: 2. Turn on each vector layer one at a time and click Show Table to open their attribute tables.

L82@P5: You do not have to rely on prepared or existing layers to enter data into your GIS system. You can add
L83@P5: point data to your GIS map in several different ways. One way is to use specific longitude and latitude
L84@P5: coordinates. In this section you will locate three cities by entering their longitude and latitude values.
L85@P5: Note that these values are in decimal degrees. Decimal degrees are degrees, minutes, and seconds
L86@P5: expressed as a decimal.

L88@P5: 2. Change the basemap to National Geographic.
L89@P5: 3. In the search bar, clear any exiting text.

L93@P5: 6. Zoom out until you can see the name of the city.
