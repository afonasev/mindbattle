L1: # Cadastral Maps
L2-L4: A cadastre is a set of records about land extent, value, and ownership or other basis of use/occupancy; a modern cadastre normally includes large-scale maps/plans and corresponding registers.
L5-L7: A cadastral map covers parcels in an area; it shows parcel boundaries and may include resources, physical structures, geology, soils, vegetation, and land use.
L8-L9: Scale is important for parcel identification; computer display at almost any scale can create an impression of accuracy greater than the survey data warrants.
L20: A cadastral map should show each parcel boundary and may also show area and boundary lengths/bearings.
L24-L29: Needed scale varies by parcel size; ordinary topographic maps are inadequate for cadastral purposes; digitized enlargement cannot add accuracy beyond source data.
L33-L37: Identifiable ground points and boundary turning-point coordinates support identifying parcel boundaries.
L40-L42: Parcel reference numbers can link a plot to survey and other records; boundary changes should be recorded.
